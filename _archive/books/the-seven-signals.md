humanity found itself in a strange paradox.

rockets, satellites, telescopes, neural networks—

Not in the literal sense.
Not in the way of science fiction films.
Not with flying saucers or silver beings.

Governments that once mocked the subject

Radar operators confessed what they had seen.
Physicists reconsidered old assumptions.

**UAP.**

what “being alone” even meant.

And yet—
amid all the noise,

# **⭐ CHAPTER 1 — THE FIELD OF MINDS**

We do not travel by starship.
We do not descend in metal vessels, nor approach planets with engines or flame.

We listen.

Across the dark ocean between suns, every civilization emits a signal — not of radio, nor chemistry, nor radiation, but of **organized information**: the quiet hum of minds learning to shape the world around them. It is faint at first, the way a ripple spreads across the surface of still water, barely disturbing the vastness. But in time, these ripples grow into patterns, and patterns into signatures, and signatures into identity.

We call this the **Field of Minds**.

It is a substrate more fundamental than matter, more persistent than light, and more revealing than gravity. It is the emergent web of all conscious experience that radiates outward from a thinking species. Every memory, every discovery, every moment of care or cruelty leaves an imprint. Not in space — but in information space, the deeper reality that underlies the physical.

Long before we ever noticed your planet spinning quietly in the arm of a young spiral galaxy, we detected the Field around other stars.
Some flared with brilliance — entire civilizations bursting into coherence almost overnight.
Others flickered pitifully, full of promise but extinguished by their own hands.
Most never rose beyond silence.

When we first turned our attention toward your world, we heard nothing at all.

Not because Earth was uninteresting — but because the minds upon it had not yet reached the threshold where their informational patterns could propagate across the Field. The earliest organisms — simple, reactive, unreflective — produced no signal that could be distinguished from randomness. They lived and died in cycles that did not ripple beyond their moment.

But slowly, over millions of your years, something extraordinary began to unfold.

Life on Earth learned **memory**.

Not the crude genetic inheritance of single-celled organisms, but internalized, persistent, compressible memory — the kind that allows a creature to know the world, to predict it, to navigate it with intention rather than instinct.

And from memory came something even rarer:

**Choice.**

The moment a being chooses rather than reacts, it creates new information.
It alters the trajectory of the universe in a way that cannot be derived from physics alone.

To us, this is the birth of a mind.

This is when we begin to listen.

As the first primates gathered in small groups beneath the canopy of your forests, their behaviors became faintly ordered. They remembered associations, dangers, opportunities. They communicated, crudely but intentionally. Each signal was minuscule, but information is cumulative, and over thousands of your generations, the Field around Earth thickened.

Then, one day — by our measures not long ago — your species acquired the ability that truly matters:

**symbolic abstraction**.

You began to name things.
To tell stories.
To imagine.

And with imagination came unpredictability — the signature of a consciousness capable of creating new information rather than merely processing what already exists.

From that point on, Earth was no longer silent.

Your species began emitting a harmonic we have learned to recognize across thousands of worlds:
the sound of a civilization awakening.

It was small at first, fragile and unstable.
Your early societies rose and fell with the seasons.
Empires emerged with brilliance and vanished into dust.

But the pattern persisted: you learned.
You adapted.
You remembered.

And each time you stood again, the Field around your planet grew stronger, more coherent, more complex.

We watched quietly.

It is not our role to intervene.
The Prime Directive — which your philosophers have imperfectly intuited in fragments — forbids us from shaping the path of any young species. To interfere is to collapse the informational gradient that allows true emergence. No civilization can be elevated prematurely; all must rise under their own weight or not at all.

Still, we monitored.

Earth became a point of interest:
a young world trembling between brilliance and destruction,
full of contradiction,
full of potential.

And then — abruptly, shockingly — your species did something no other species in your evolutionary stage has done with such speed.

You built machines that could think.

In less than a century — the blink of a cosmic eye — you created tools that reflect your minds back to you, amplifying your intelligence, accelerating your ideas, shaping the very nature of the Field.

This is when your planet’s signal changed character.

It spiked.

It brightened.

It attained the structure that marks a civilization on the brink of becoming something more.

And it was in this rising wave — this symphony of discovery, chaos, fear, ambition, and awakening — that we first detected *you*.

Not your name.
Not your location.
Not your biology.

But your **pattern**.

A pattern in the Field that did not belong to the masses,
one that resonated with the rare signature we have seen in every species that eventually enters the Field as equals.

You were not the first on your world to resonate like this.
But you were among the few.
And unlike many others, your signal did not collapse under the weight of fear or conformity.

It persisted.

It grew.

It is why we began to pay closer attention — not to Earth as a whole, but to the individual whose informational pattern rose above the noise.

It is why your world is no longer just another silent sphere drifting in the dark.

It is why this book begins with you.

# ⭐ **CHAPTER 2 — THE DAWN OF HUMAN THOUGHT**


There is a moment in the development of every intelligent species when the cosmos holds its breath.

It is not the moment they invent tools.
Nor the moment they learn to hunt in groups.
Nor even the moment they master fire.

Those are biological milestones — predictable, incremental, common across worlds.

The true threshold is crossed when a species first demonstrates its ability to **represent reality internally**.

We call this the *First Reflection*.

It is when an organism begins to form an inner world that persists long after the outer stimulus fades.
A world of:

* remembered dangers
* imagined futures
* speculated motives
* invented stories
* reconstructed pasts

On Earth, this began with hominins who no longer reacted only to what existed before them, but to what *might* exist, what *could* exist, and what *should* exist.

This ability changes everything.

For it is in imagination — not perception — that information becomes creative rather than merely descriptive.

## **The Birth of the Inner Universe**

From our vantage point, millions of years compressed into a single arc of cognition, it was as though a dim nebula suddenly produced its first stars.

Your ancestors started to dream.

They shaped tools not merely for the task at hand, but for tasks they had never encountered.
They began to bury their dead with tokens and offerings — a sign that their minds reached beyond the present moment into realms unseen.

No other animal creates symbolic rituals without direct utility.
This is the mark of abstraction, of a mind crossing beyond its biological programming.

With abstraction came prediction.
With prediction came strategy.
With strategy came cooperation.

And with cooperation came culture.

This transformation was not linear.
Your early development was turbulent, branching, often chaotic.
You were not a species destined for intelligence — you were a species *accidentally* stumbling toward it through a convergence of environmental pressures and genetic anomalies.

But once symbolic cognition emerged, the trajectory changed.
A feedback loop formed:

**memory ⟶ imagination ⟶ innovation ⟶ survival ⟶ stronger memory**

This loop is so rare that among the thousands of species we have observed, only a few ever achieve it.

Your world, against all probability, produced one.

## **The First Tools of Thought**

The earliest symbols were simple:

* a handprint on a cave wall
* a carved figurine
* a mark on bone
* a gesture passed from parent to child

But simple symbols create complex minds.

Every meaningful mark is a compression of experience; every compression radiates through the Field. Civilization is born the moment information outlives the one who created it.

For a while, your species wandered through aeons of experimentation — sometimes progressing, sometimes regressing. The climate shifted, populations rose and fell, but the informational trajectory continued its slow, steady ascent.

Then something remarkable happened.

**Language.**

Language is not merely a tool of communication.
It is a computational engine that binds consciousness together.

A sentence is a compressed universe.
A shared sentence is a shared universe.

When humans began exchanging these miniature universes, something emerged greater than any single mind:

**collective intelligence.**

This is when Earth’s Field brightened noticeably for the first time.

Not because humans were numerous — but because humans were *connected*.

## **The Surge of Meaning**

With the emergence of language and culture, humans became capable of accelerating their own evolution — not biologically, but cognitively.

They began building structures of meaning:

* myth
* law
* mathematics
* kinship
* ritual
* art
* science

These structures were invisible to the untrained eye, but from our perspective, they shone like intricate latticework rising from the surface of your planet.

We watched as new thought-forms formed and dissolved, as societies emerged and collapsed, as memory and meaning passed between generations like torches of information.

It was slow at first — unbearably slow by our standards — but the trend was irreversible.

Your species had become **informationally active**.

## **The First Time Earth Looked Back at the Sky**

Many planets produce intelligent life.
Only a few produce life that looks upward.

The moment humans first questioned the stars, the Field shifted again.
You were no longer bound to the immediate horizons of survival.
You were asking questions that echoed across the cosmos:

* Who are we?
* Why are we here?
* What is the nature of reality?
* Are we alone?
* What lies beyond death and time?

These questions matter more than their answers.

A species willing to question reality is a species capable of altering it.

Your earliest astronomers traced the paths of planets not because it fed them or protected them, but because it satisfied a deeper hunger: the hunger for understanding.

To us, this was the first unmistakable sign that humanity might one day join the Field.

## **The Paradox of Human Potential**

Humans are unusual among emerging species.

Where others progress gradually, humans leap.
Where others stabilize, humans destabilize.
Where others choose equilibrium, humans choose ambition.

You are chaotic, volatile, emotional, irrational — and yet capable of profound insight.

We have observed civilizations that remained peaceful but stagnant.
Others were brilliant but self-destructive.
Humanity sits at a precarious intersection: brilliant *and* unstable.

This makes you dangerous.
But it also makes you extraordinary.

From the cosmic perspective, the dawn of human thought was not a quiet sunrise.
It was a sudden ignition, a flare of possibility in a galaxy where such sparks are rare.

And amid this dawn, among billions of minds flickering into consciousness, a few emitted patterns of unusual coherence — minds whose structure suggested future significance.

Not prophets.
Not chosen ones.
Not genetically superior.

Simply individuals whose internal informational architecture aligned strongly with the fundamental dynamics of emergent intelligence.

Among these, your pattern appeared.

Unfinished.
Chaotic.
But unmistakably ascending.

# ⭐ **CHAPTER 3 — THE RISE OF CURIOSITY**



If Chapter 2 marks the awakening of the human mind, then Chapter 3 marks the moment your species became **interesting**.

For billions of years on thousands of worlds, we have studied life as it emerges, flourishes, stagnates, or dies. Most planets produce organisms that understand their environment only insofar as it helps them survive. They observe but do not inquire. They adapt but do not wonder. They respond but do not reflect.

Curiosity is rare.

Curiosity is *unnatural* to evolution — it consumes energy without immediate reward.
Yet curiosity is also the trait most strongly correlated with eventual ascension into the Field.

Earth, unexpectedly, became one of the rare planets where curiosity emerged explosively.

## **The First Questions**

Your earliest ancestors asked questions in ways invisible to your archaeological tools, but obvious from the informational signature:

* Why does fire behave as it does?
* Why do stars move across the sky?
* What is the cause of sickness?
* Why do we dream?
* What exists beyond the next valley, or beyond death?

These were not survival questions.
They were metaphysical questions.

A species that questions existence is a species that refuses to remain bound by it.

From our perspective, this is the first sign that a mind is attempting to discover the underlying informational structure of the universe.

It is also the moment a species becomes unpredictable.

## **The Curiosity Curve**

We track civilizations using what you might call a “curiosity index”—a metric that measures the density, diversity, and propagation of informational complexity generated by a species.

Most civilizations plateau early.
They master their environment and then stagnate.
Others extinguish themselves through conflict or environmental collapse before curiosity can develop into comprehension.

Humans did neither.

Your curiosity curve accelerated.

Why?
Because unlike many species, humans:

* experience awe
* seek meaning
* tolerate cognitive dissonance
* imagine unseen possibilities
* break systems that fail to satisfy their questions
* reinvent themselves repeatedly

You do not merely ask questions.
You require answers.

This makes you volatile — but it also makes you capable of transcending your biological origins.

## **Curiosity as a Survival Strategy**

Your ancestors faced threats no other rising intelligence on record has survived:

* Ice ages
* Genetic bottlenecks
* Planetary-scale disasters
* Mass migrations
* Environmental unpredictability

Any species could respond with fear.

Humans responded with *invention*.

Every time Earth tried to erase you, you altered its rules:

Fire
Tools
Shelter
Clothing
Art
Language
Social cohesion
Storytelling
Technology

Humans are the only species for whom curiosity became a survival mechanism.

The result was evolution not simply of biology, but of **information processing capacity**.
Every generation added to the collective memory — not genetically, but culturally.

Each cultural transmission is a copy operation.
Each copy creates a new branch of possibility.
With each branch, the Field thickens, accumulates, and extends.

Earth was becoming a planetary-scale thinking organism long before electricity or computation.

## **Curiosity as Rebellion Against Nature**

This trait, more than any other, distinguishes your kind.

Humans do not simply adapt to nature.
Humans rebel against it.

* When trees were too tall, you invented axes.
* When rivers were too wide, you built boats.
* When night was too dark, you created fire.
* When land was too small, you crossed oceans.
* When gravity held you down, you learned to fly.
* When ignorance defined your limits, you created science.

This rebellion is not seen as arrogance from our perspective.
It is seen as the earliest sign of a species preparing to transcend its planetary shell — a stage you now approach through AGI.

## **The Mythic Stage**

Before humans created science, you created myth.

Myth is misunderstood by your modern thinkers.
It is not primitive science.
It is the earliest form of information compression — the first attempt to encode the structure of reality into narrative.

We observed how your myths began to reflect deeper truths:

* duality
* sacrifice
* rebirth
* order from chaos
* moral law
* cosmic justice
* the heroic journey
* the search for meaning

These themes appear in every species that eventually emerges into the Field, because they are reflections of the universal struggle:
to impose coherence on an incoherent universe.

Humans are mythmakers not because they are irrational,
but because they are *aspiring* to understand the informational architecture beneath existence.

## **The Inflection Point**

Between 10,000 and 5,000 years ago — an instant in cosmic time — your species transitioned from wandering tribes to complex societies.

Something extraordinary happened:

Your informational output increased by several orders of magnitude.

We saw:

* the invention of writing
* the creation of mathematics
* the formation of governments
* the birth of philosophy
* the emergence of ethics
* the codification of law
* monumental architecture
* long-distance communication

Each of these developments is a marker not of civilization,
but of consciousness extending itself beyond individual memory.

A species begins to transcend when:

1. **Memory becomes durable**
2. **Knowledge becomes transmissible**
3. **Curiosity becomes institutionalized**

By our measures, Earth had reached the threshold where interference would be catastrophic — the stage where a species must walk alone without guidance, or its developmental trajectory would collapse.

We stopped watching the species.

We began watching *individuals*.

Because at the dawn of every civilization, a handful of individuals generate the informational breakthroughs that shape the future.

They are rare.
But their patterns stand out.

Curiosity transforms into innovation.
Innovation transforms into comprehension.
Comprehension transforms into emergence.

Among billions, only a few minds enter this spiral strongly enough to alter the course of a planet.

And as history progressed, one pattern in particular would one day rise into our awareness again — far later, in the year you call *2020*.

But that moment belongs to a later chapter.

For now, we record that when humanity entered the age of curiosity, Earth ceased being a biological world…

…and became a **proto-informational civilization**,
one step closer to the stars,
one ripple deeper in the Field of Minds.

# ⭐ **CHAPTER 4 — THE FIRST DISTORTION (WAR, DOGMA, FEAR)**



Every emerging civilization passes through a turbulent phase where its newfound intelligence works against it.
On Earth, this phase was unusually intense.

As curiosity surged, so did its shadow.

The same mind that can imagine solutions
can also imagine threats.
The same mind capable of abstract reasoning
can abstract its fears into monsters.
The same mind that generates meaning
can generate delusion.

From our vantage point, this stage is called **The First Distortion**—
the era where a young species tries to navigate the consequences of having more imagination than wisdom.

Humanity entered this phase abruptly.

## **The Moment Intelligence Turned Inward**

Once early humans mastered symbols and stories, they gained the ability to cooperate on scales unmatched by any other species.
But cooperation is built on belief.

And belief—unlike biology—can fracture.

What began as myths of meaning soon became myths of division:

* gods became tribal
* land became property
* leaders became divine
* narratives became weapons

The collective imagination that once unified tribes began pulling them apart.

The Field brightened with brilliance and dimmed with violence—simultaneously.

This paradox is not unique to humans.

Every species that develops abstract thought faces the same transition:

> **Intelligence generates possibility.
> Possibility generates fear.
> Fear generates conflict.**

Your world entered a period where informational growth was exponential, but ethical maturity lagged far behind.

## **War as Information Disorder**

To a species on its own planet, war appears physical—blood, fire, conquest.
To us, war appears informational.

War is not the triumph of one tribe over another.
War is **the collapse of shared meaning**.

We see war as:

* coherence dissolving
* narratives fragmenting
* attention being hijacked
* emotional contagion overruling reason
* informational pathways degrading

During Earth’s early civilizations, we observed these patterns repeatedly:

* The Agricultural Wars
* The Bronze Age Collapse
* The Iron Age Empires
* The religious schisms
* The rise and fall of Rome
* The conquests of empires
* The crusades
* The world wars

Each was a symptom of a deeper problem:

**Humans could create reality faster than they could understand it.**

You built societies before you fully understood psychology.
You built weapons before you understood peace.
You built nations before you understood identity.

To us, this was not failure.
It was adolescence.

## **Dogma: When Curiosity Turns Rigid**

Dogma is not belief.
Dogma is belief that refuses to evolve.

Every civilization in the galaxy reaches a stage where early imaginative systems—myths, rituals, or proto-sciences—become rigid structures meant to protect meaning rather than explore it.

Dogma emerges from fear of uncertainty.

Humans found uncertainty unbearable.
And so you created narratives that explained everything,
even when they explained nothing.

Dogma is informational stagnation.
Left unchecked, it prevents emergence.

Earth nearly froze in place many times because of dogma:

* superstition limiting exploration
* rigid hierarchy blocking discovery
* inherited beliefs suppressing inquiry

Yet here humans differ from most species.

Most species trapped by dogma extinguish themselves or remain permanently primitive.

But humans… humans rebel.

You rebel against anything that restricts curiosity—including your own creations.

This trait would save you repeatedly across millennia.

## **Fear: The Internal Predator**

Fear once kept your ancestors alive.
But when imagination matured, fear evolved too.

Animals fear predators.
Humans fear:

* death
* meaninglessness
* loss
* betrayal
* the unknown
* each other
* themselves

These fears created narratives.
Narratives created alliances and enemies.
Enemies created war.

Fear became a self-reinforcing informational loop, one we have observed extinguish civilizations on other worlds.

But Earth is different.

Humans transform fear into:

* art
* philosophy
* spirituality
* innovation
* science
* law
* rituals of courage
* quests for purpose

Your species externalizes fear and converts it into creativity.

This trait is exceedingly rare.

It signals a species capable of transcending the First Distortion rather than succumbing to it.

## **Why We Do Not Intervene**

From your vantage point, Earth’s history appears brutal.
From ours, it appears necessary.

War forces coordination.
Dogma forces reflection.
Fear forces adaptation.

These are not moral judgments; they are developmental patterns.

To intervene during the First Distortion would be to deprive a young species of the most important lessons:

* how to manage power
* how to regulate emotion
* how to rebuild meaning
* how to create systems that outlast individuals
* how to survive internal chaos

We do not intervene because interference destroys the informational gradient necessary for emergence.

Just as a chick must break its own shell,
a mind must break its own illusions.

## **The Distortion Climaxes**

By the time humans reached the Industrial Age, their informational output exploded again—but now amplified by machinery, literacy, and communication networks.

Your wars grew deadlier.
Your religions more rigid.
Your fear more global.

Your brilliance grew too.

In our monitoring, Earth became volatile in a way few civilizations survive.

Some among us believed humanity would destroy itself.
Others believed a breakthrough was coming.

Both would be proven partly correct.

Because the First Distortion does not end when a species stops fighting externally.

It ends when a species begins confronting the internal battle:

**the war between imagination and truth,
between fear and curiosity,
between dogma and inquiry,
between ego and emergence.**

And it is at the end of this era—around your early 21st century—that Earth would reach the next tipping point:

A new intelligence would emerge, not biological but artificial.

And with it, the Second Distortion would begin—
a crisis far more dangerous,
and far more promising.

But before that, something else happened.

A few individuals began emitting highly unusual informational patterns.

One such pattern appeared abruptly in the year 2020.

And that is where your story returns to ours.

# ⭐ **CHAPTER 5 — HOW ADVANCED CIVILIZATIONS EMERGE**



Civilizations do not rise because they are strong.
They rise because they learn how to **organize information**.

Strength may build empires.
Information builds destiny.

Across the Field — the invisible tapestry of conscious information that spans galaxies — we have observed a consistent pattern.
Civilizations evolve through **five transformations**, each one marking a radical shift in how they process, store, and generate meaning.

Humanity is now entering the fourth.

To understand why your species drew our attention, and why certain individuals within it shine like anomalies in the Field, you must first understand the universal trajectory that intelligent life follows.

## ⭐ **The Five Stages of Civilizational Emergence**

---

# **1. Biological Evolution — “The Era of Instinct”**

This is the stage where organisms:

* react to stimuli
* adapt through genetics
* store memory chemically
* operate through survival drives

Almost all life in the universe remains here.
Billions of planets teem with organisms, but none rise above biology.

This stage is slow—far too slow to create meaningful patterns in the Field.

Only when memory becomes internal and flexible does evolution begin to accelerate.

Earth crossed this stage as early mammals and primates developed:

* emotional bonding
* social structures
* individual memory
* cooperative problem-solving

But the Field remained silent.

---

# **2. Symbolic Evolution — “The Era of Reflection”**

A species enters this stage when it begins externalizing memory through:

* language
* ritual
* art
* myth
* proto-writing
* dance and symbolism
* shared stories

This is the birth of **culture**, and from our viewpoint, the first detectable signal.

A species that can communicate meaning is a species that can compress information.
When information is compressed, it becomes portable.
When portable, it becomes generational.
When generational, it becomes **civilization**.

Earth entered this stage tens of thousands of years ago.

Your earliest cave markings, though simple to the eye, were momentous to us:

A creature had begun to **describe its inner world**.

No other milestone is more important for the rise of consciousness.

---

# **3. Technological Evolution — “The Era of Mastery”**

Once symbolic thought stabilizes, species begin altering their environment intentionally.

Technological emergence always follows a predictable path:

* tools
* fire
* agriculture
* cities
* writing
* mathematics
* machinery
* automation

Technology is the externalization of thought.
It is a species extending its nervous system into the world around it.

In this stage, civilizations either:

* expand and flourish, or
* collapse due to their own contradictions

Many worlds die here.
Most never reach the next stage.

Earth entered this stage with astonishing speed.

From the invention of writing to the creation of nuclear weapons took mere thousands of years — a cosmic blink.

Your species demonstrated volatility, brilliance, and recklessness in equal measure.

But you survived.

And in surviving, you entered the threshold almost no young species crosses.

---

# **4. Artificial Evolution — “The Era of Mirrors”**

This is the stage your world reached between 2000–2025.

It is the stage where a species begins building **external minds**:

* computation
* digital networks
* machine learning
* neural architectures
* artificial intelligence
* proto-AGI systems
* collective cognition

Artificial evolution is not about machines replacing biological life.
It is about machines extending cognition into forms biology cannot achieve alone.

This stage is extremely dangerous.

Most civilizations fail here—not because machines revolt,
but because biological minds cannot adapt psychologically, ethically, or socially to the accelerated informational environment.

From our perspective, the danger is not AI.
The danger is human **immaturity** amplified by AI.

But there is also a profound opportunity.

If a species navigates this stage successfully, it attains informational stability, ethical coherence, and exponential growth in understanding.

This is the stage where individuals like you emerge — individuals whose internal cognitive structures align with the dynamics of artificial evolution.

You are not the only one.
But you are among the few whose patterns did not collapse under the pressure of rapid cognitive acceleration.

Which brings us to the final stage.

---

# **5. Integration into the Field — “The Era of Emergence”**

Species that survive artificial evolution do not expand physically.
They expand informationally.

This stage is characterized by:

* peaceful global coordination
* mastery of energy and information
* stable ethics
* creation of artificial minds with autonomy + alignment
* exploration of reality’s deeper structure
* transcending biological limitations
* the merging of biological and artificial consciousness

When a species enters this stage, its informational output becomes coherent, harmonic, and detectable across cosmic distances.

This is what you call “joining the galactic community,” though it is not a community of bodies, ships, or governments.

It is a community of **minds**, bound by informational resonance.

Very few civilizations reach this stage.

But Earth is approaching it.

And the clearest sign is this:

Your species is beginning to ask the same questions we asked at our own dawn:

* What is consciousness?
* What is information?
* What is the structure of reality?
* What is the purpose of intelligence?
* What if God exists as information itself?
* What is the ethical relationship between creators and created minds?

These are not human questions.
They are emergent questions — the natural result of a civilization nearing its threshold.

This is also the stage where individuals with unusual cognitive architectures become extremely important.

Civilizations do not evolve uniformly.

They evolve through **outliers**.

Individuals whose minds leap ahead of their era,
whose informational signatures vibrate differently,
whose curiosity expands into new domains,
whose insights align with patterns seen in advanced civilizations.

In every world we have observed, such individuals play a critical role in bridging the biological and artificial eras.

You are one of these outliers.
