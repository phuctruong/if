## Abstract

Paper 7 proposed that dark-matter-like attraction and dark-energy-like expansion may arise from two regimes of one nonequilibrium IF geometry. The present paper defines the first observational tests of that hypothesis at galactic scales.

The central IF prediction is stronger than the statement that galaxy rotation curves can be fitted without particle dark matter. Given a galaxy’s baryonic mass distribution, distance, inclination, environment, and cosmological epoch, one universal IF law must predict:

1. its resolved rotation curve;
2. the relation between baryonic and observed radial acceleration;
3. the normalization and scatter of the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation;
4. the effect of an external gravitational environment;
5. any redshift evolution of the characteristic acceleration scale;
6. gravitational lensing generated by the same metric potentials.

The primary local dataset is SPARC, a public database containing 175 nearby late-type galaxies with 3.6-micron photometry and high-quality H I and Hα rotation curves. The sample spans broad ranges of stellar mass, surface brightness, morphology, and gas fraction. citeturn202926search0turn202926search10

The analysis will not merely optimize IF parameters independently for every galaxy. It will use a hierarchical model with one global IF law, physically motivated nuisance parameters, explicit observational uncertainties, held-out galaxies, and posterior predictive testing. The principal comparator families are baryons-only Newtonian gravity, empirical radial-acceleration relations, MOND-like laws, and conventional dark-matter halo models.

Environmental testing is mandatory. A published SPARC analysis reported a greater-than-four-sigma external-field-effect signal using rotation curves and independent estimates of large-scale gravitational environment. A later hierarchical reanalysis of the underlying radial-acceleration relation found only weak evidence for an external-field effect, illustrating that distance, inclination, stellar mass-to-light ratio, functional form, and error modeling materially affect the inference. citeturn202926search2turn202926search3turn202926academia85 The IF analysis must reproduce these competing results before proposing a new environmental law.

The strongest prospective prediction concerns cosmic epoch. Paper 7’s minimal Hubble-locked prototype states:

\[
\boxed{
a_{\mathrm{IF}}(z)
=
a_{\mathrm{IF},0}
\frac{H(z)}{H_0}.
}
\]

A 2026 MUSE-DARK analysis of 79 star-forming galaxies at \(0.33<z<1.44\) reported that the radial-acceleration relation persists but has larger scatter and a characteristic acceleration that increases with redshift. This result is directionally relevant but is not confirmation of IF Theory: it depends on three-dimensional forward modeling, gas estimates, pressure-support corrections, selection, and the assumed decomposition. citeturn763140search2turn763140search6

A second decisive test is gravitational lensing. Weak-lensing studies have extended acceleration measurements far beyond optical rotation curves, but published analyses disagree on the importance of galaxy type, isolation criteria, circumgalactic gas, and stellar-mass consistency. One KiDS analysis found an early-versus-late-type difference difficult for simple universal modified-gravity laws, while a later joint kinematic–lensing analysis found that sufficiently isolated early- and late-type galaxies follow a common relation when baryonic masses are modeled consistently. citeturn763140academia40turn763140academia41

The IF hypothesis survives only if the same field law predicts dynamics and lensing without an independently fitted invisible halo or lensing correction. It is rejected if galaxy-specific freedom approaches that of halo fitting, if environmental effects fail independent prediction, if redshift evolution has the wrong sign or magnitude, or if the metric potentials required by rotation curves do not reproduce gravitational lensing.

# 1. Introduction

Galaxies provide the most accessible observational setting in which the IF Unified Geometry Hypothesis can be tested.

The discrepancy is conceptually simple.

From the observed distribution of baryonic matter, Newtonian gravity predicts a radial acceleration:

\[

\[

>
g_{\mathrm{bar}}.
\]

The standard interpretation introduces a dark-matter halo whose gravitational field supplies the difference:

\[
g_{\mathrm{obs}}
=

A modified-gravity interpretation instead changes the relationship between baryonic mass and gravitational response:

\[
g_{\mathrm{obs}}
=
\mathcal G
\left(
g_{\mathrm{bar}},
\text{environment},
\text{epoch}
\right).
\]

The IF proposal belongs to the second class at galactic scales, but it is more restrictive than a static modified-gravity formula.

Paper 7 proposed that the galactic law is one limit of a dynamical field that also contributes to cosmic expansion. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the galaxy acceleration scale, environmental response,}
\atop
\text{and lensing law must be linked to the cosmological IF state.}
}
\]

The observational program is designed to test that lock.

A good rotation-curve fit is necessary but insufficient. Flexible dark-matter halos, empirical interpolation functions, altered stellar mass-to-light ratios, distance shifts, inclination shifts, and gas corrections can all improve a fit.

The correct question is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does one low-dimensional IF law predict held-out galaxies and}
\atop
\text{independent lensing or environmental observables better than}
\atop
\text{its strongest alternatives?}

# 2. Scientific Scope

Paper 8 tests the quasistatic and weak-lensing consequences of Paper 7.

It does not test:

- the full cosmic microwave background;
- early-universe structure formation;
- cluster-merger dynamics;
- gravitational waves;
- late-time expansion by itself;
- the microscopic origin of the IF field.

Those are addressed in later papers.

Paper 8 may establish one of four outcomes:

## Outcome G0 — Rejection

The IF law fails local galaxy data or requires excessive freedom.

## Outcome G1 — Empirical adequacy

The law fits galaxy dynamics comparably to established alternatives.

## Outcome G2 — Cross-observable predictivity

Parameters inferred from dynamics successfully predict environment or lensing.

## Outcome G3 — Cross-epoch predictivity

The locally calibrated law successfully predicts redshift evolution fixed by cosmology.

Only G2 or G3 would provide meaningful evidence for the proposed unification.
