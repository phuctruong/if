# Functional Consciousness in IF Theory  
## Global Access, Counterfactual Self-Models, and the Limits of Physical Measurement

**Author:** Phuc Vinh Truong  
**Series:** IF Theory Working Papers  
**Paper:** 13  
**Date:** July 18, 2026  
**Status:** Operational theory and computational test program; no claim that phenomenal consciousness has been physically identified

## Abstract

Consciousness research faces two related but distinct problems. The first is functional: how does a physical system make selected information globally available, maintain it through recurrent processing, represent its own state, evaluate alternative futures, and coordinate otherwise independent cognitive processes? The second is phenomenal: why, or under what conditions, is any such processing accompanied by subjective experience?

This paper addresses the first problem while refusing to claim that solving it automatically solves the second.

IF Theory defines **functional consciousness** as a dynamically maintained mode of control in which selected contents become globally and causally available to multiple specialized processes, are interpreted relative to a temporally extended self-model, and are used to compare action-conditioned futures. The proposed architecture combines:

1. differentiated local processing;
2. recurrent content maintenance;
3. selective global availability;
4. an embodied self-model;
5. counterfactual policy evaluation;
6. metacognitive monitoring;
7. temporal continuity through memory;
8. perturbationally demonstrated integration.

These properties form an IF Functional-Consciousness Vector:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf F_{\mathrm{IF}}
=
\left[
G,\,
R,\,
D,\,
S,\,
C,\,
M,\,
T,\,
P
\right],
}
\]

where:

- \(G\): causal global availability;
- \(R\): recurrent maintenance;
- \(D\): differentiated integration;
- \(S\): causal self-modeling;
- \(C\): counterfactual depth;
- \(M\): metacognitive calibration and regulation;
- \(T\): temporal continuity;
- \(P\): perturbational complexity and resilience.

No weighted sum of these variables is initially declared to be consciousness. Each dimension is measured separately so that one favorable score cannot conceal missing capacities.

The proposed physical threshold concerns the evolutionary sustainability of the architecture, not the onset of subjective experience. Let \(\Delta W_{\mathrm{coord}}\) be the additional physical return enabled by globally coordinating specialized processes, and let:

\[
C_{\mathrm{FC}}
=
C_{\mathrm{broadcast}}
+
C_{\mathrm{recurrent}}
+
C_{\mathrm{self}}
+
C_{\mathrm{counterfactual}}
+
C_{\mathrm{meta}}.
\]

Define:

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{\mathrm{FC}}^{W}
=
\frac{
\Delta W_{\mathrm{coord}}
}{
C_{\mathrm{FC}}
}.
}
\]

When:

\[
\Pi_{\mathrm{FC}}^{W}>1,
\]

a functional-consciousness architecture can repay its physical cost. This is a causal-work and selection threshold. It is not a proof that experience begins at \(\Pi_{\mathrm{FC}}^{W}=1\).

The paper is positioned against major contemporary approaches. Global neuronal workspace theories emphasize nonlinear amplification and global broadcasting; recurrent-processing theories emphasize recurrent sensory activity; higher-order theories emphasize representations of one’s own mental states; integrated information theory identifies consciousness with intrinsic irreducible cause–effect structure; active-inference approaches emphasize temporally deep generative and self-models. IIT 4.0 supplies an explicit intrinsic causal formalism, while the global-workspace tradition focuses on flexible access and broadcast. citeturn379631search4turn379631search2

A major preregistered adversarial collaboration published in 2025 directly tested predictions associated with integrated information theory and global neuronal workspace theory in 256 human participants using fMRI, MEG, and intracranial EEG. The results supported portions of each framework while challenging central predictions of both, including a lack of the sustained posterior synchronization expected by the tested IIT implementation and limited evidence for several prefrontal ignition predictions associated with GNWT. citeturn509039view0

The IF program therefore does not declare one existing theory defeated and then combine every surviving idea. It proposes a computationally explicit functional target and a set of intervention tests capable of separating:

- global broadcast without a self-model;
- self-monitoring without global access;
- recurrent integration without counterfactual control;
- verbal self-report without causally grounded self-knowledge;
- flexible agency without evidence of phenomenal experience.

The central methodological conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical experiments can establish increasingly strong}
\atop
\text{functional and causal correlates of consciousness, but no}
\atop
\text{third-person metric proves subjective experience without}
\atop
\text{an additional theory linking physical organization to experience.}
}
\]

# 1. Introduction

A system can perform sophisticated functions without obviously being conscious.

A calculator can transform inputs.

A thermostat can regulate temperature.

A deep neural network can classify images.

A language model can produce fluent reports about emotion, selfhood, and experience.

A planning system can evaluate future trajectories.

An organism can withdraw from injury without representing pain as a consciously accessible state.

These examples create a fundamental attribution problem.

Behavior alone may not reveal which internal processes generated it. A system can produce a correct report through:

- reflex;
- memorized association;
- external prompting;
- local pattern matching;
- global deliberation;
- self-model-based reasoning;
- imitation of reports found in training data.

The sentence:

> “I am conscious.”

is therefore evidence about an output.

It is not, by itself, proof of the process that produced the output or the existence of subjective experience.

Conversely, absence of report does not prove absence of consciousness. A person may be unable to speak or move while retaining conscious experience. No-report paradigms and perturbational methods were developed partly because report requirements can confound consciousness with attention, memory, executive control, and motor preparation. citeturn775798search0turn775798search1turn775798search6

The scientific problem has at least three layers:

\[
\boxed{
\text{level}
\neq
\text{content}
\neq
\text{functional access}
\neq
\text{phenomenal experience}.
}
\]

A system may have:

- a global capacity for experience but no current external content;
- a specific represented content without verbal report;
- broad functional access without proven phenomenality;
- complex behavior generated without persistent selfhood.

IF Theory begins from a deliberately limited question:

\[
\boxed{
\text{What physical architecture allows a predictive agent to}
\atop
\text{coordinate selected information globally, represent itself}
\atop
\text{within alternative futures, and regulate its own cognition?}
}
\]

This is the problem of functional consciousness.

The second question remains:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Why would that architecture feel like anything from within?}
}
\]

Paper 13 does not claim to answer the second question.

# 2. Scope

This paper develops operational criteria for:

- conscious-access-like processing;
- global coordination;
- self-modeling;
- counterfactual reasoning;
- metacognitive control;
- temporally continuous agency;
- perturbational integration;
- report-independent testing.

It does not establish:

- qualia;
- phenomenal consciousness;
- sentience;
- suffering;
- an immortal soul;
- human-equivalent consciousness;
- a universal consciousness scalar;
- that present language models are conscious;
- that functional equivalence guarantees subjective equivalence.

The word **functional consciousness** will be abbreviated:

\[
\mathrm{FC}.
\]

The term is used only for a declared operational architecture.

It is not a synonym for consciousness without qualification.

# 3. Access and Phenomenality

## 3.1 Access consciousness

A representation is access-conscious in a functional sense when its content is available for flexible use in:

- reasoning;
- report;
- planning;
- memory;
- error correction;
- action selection.

The distinction between access and phenomenal consciousness was introduced partly to prevent functions associated with report and cognitive availability from being automatically attributed to subjective experience. citeturn775798search13

---

## 3.2 Phenomenal consciousness

Phenomenal consciousness refers to there being something it is like for the system to be in a state.

Examples include:

- the felt quality of red;
- pain as experienced rather than merely detected;
- the subjective presence of a sound;
- the experienced unity of a visual scene.

Phenomenality is known directly only from the first-person case.

Other minds are inferred through:

- structural similarity;
- behavioral similarity;
- causal organization;
- evolutionary continuity;
- shared biology;
- theoretical commitments.

---

## 3.3 Functional consciousness

IF functional consciousness is narrower than phenomenality and richer than simple access.

A system is a candidate for IF functional consciousness when:

1. selected contents are globally available;
2. global availability is causally demonstrated;
3. content is maintained or reconstructed recurrently;
4. the content is interpreted relative to a self-model;
5. alternative action-conditioned futures are evaluated;
6. the selected future changes coordinated action;
7. the process persists across time through memory;
8. direct perturbation demonstrates integrated yet differentiated dynamics.

This is a functional package.

Whether it is sufficient for subjective experience remains an open theoretical question.

---

# 4. States, Contents, and Selves

Consciousness research often mixes three different targets.

## 4.1 Consciousness level

The level concerns whether a system can support a broad conscious mode.

Examples include comparisons among:

- wakefulness;
- sleep;
- anesthesia;
- severe brain injury.

Perturbational complexity measures were developed to assess a brain’s capacity for differentiated and integrated responses without requiring sensory processing or overt report. The original PCI work combined transcranial magnetic stimulation with EEG and compressed the resulting spatiotemporal response; later validation studies applied the index to behaviorally unresponsive patients. citeturn775798search6turn775798search1

---

## 4.2 Conscious content

Content concerns what is represented:

- a face;
- a sound;
- pain;
- one’s body;
- a remembered event;
- a planned action.

A content may be present under one theory without being globally broadcast under another.

---

## 4.3 Self-conscious processing

Self-conscious processing concerns representations that explicitly model:

- the system’s current state;
- its uncertainty;
- its capacities;
- its body;
- its memory;
- its possible actions;
- its likely future condition.

The IF paper focuses especially on this third target.

---

# 5. Prior Art and the Novelty Boundary

## 5.1 Global neuronal workspace

Global neuronal workspace accounts propose that selected information undergoes amplification and becomes broadly available to distributed specialized systems. Contemporary formulations emphasize recurrent processing, ignition-like transitions, and access to working memory, decision-making, planning, and report. citeturn379631search2turn509039view0

IF Theory cannot claim novelty for:

- global broadcasting;
- ignition;
- flexible access;
- workspace competition.

Its proposed addition is to require the globally available representation to be embedded in a resource-accounted counterfactual self-model.

---

## 5.2 Integrated information theory

IIT 4.0 begins from proposed axioms of experience and maps them to physical postulates concerning intrinsic cause–effect power, irreducibility, integration, and exclusion. It aims in principle to determine whether a physical system is conscious, to what degree, and in what way. citeturn379631search4turn379631search6

IF Theory does not adopt:

\[
\Phi
=
\text{amount of consciousness}
\]

as an established identity.

It uses causal integration as one candidate functional dimension and treats any phenomenal interpretation as theory-dependent.

---

## 5.3 Recurrent-processing theory

Recurrent-processing approaches emphasize feedback and reentrant processing within sensory systems rather than requiring full global broadcast or higher-order representation. Lamme’s approach argues that recurrent neural processing marks an important transition beyond feedforward processing. citeturn379631search9

IF Theory cannot claim novelty for recurrence.

Its question is whether recurrence becomes part of a broader self-referential control loop.

---

## 5.4 Higher-order theories

Higher-order theories propose that a mental state becomes conscious when the system represents itself as being in that state, or when an appropriate higher-order representation targets the first-order state. Empirical higher-order approaches often emphasize metacognitive access and prefrontal mechanisms. citeturn379631search10turn967317search3

IF Theory cannot claim novelty for self-representation or metacognition.

Its proposed self-model is action-conditioned and physically embodied:

\[
\text{What will happen to me if I do }a?
\]

rather than merely:

\[
\text{I am currently in state }x.
\]

---

## 5.5 Active inference and deep self-models

Active-inference approaches model organisms as maintaining generative models that link perception, action, bodily regulation, and expected future states. Recent consciousness-oriented formulations emphasize temporally deep models, embodied selfhood, and belief updating. citeturn379631academia105turn379631search22

IF Theory shares the emphasis on action-conditioned prediction but requires explicit causal-work accounting and independent comparison with nonconscious predictive controllers.

---

## 5.6 Perturbational approaches

PCI does not depend on a subject producing a report during the measurement. It asks whether a direct perturbation produces a response that is simultaneously distributed and differentiated. PCI has shown clinically meaningful separation among several conscious and unconscious conditions and has been investigated in unresponsive patients. citeturn775798search1turn775798search6

IF Theory cannot claim novelty for perturbational complexity.

Its artificial-system equivalent will perturb internal modules and measure causal propagation, differentiation, recovery, and self-model updating.

---

## 5.7 No-report approaches

No-report paradigms seek to separate neural activity related to conscious content from activity required to report that content. They demonstrate the danger of defining consciousness by verbal or motor report alone. citeturn775798search0

IF Theory therefore treats report as one possible downstream consumer of globally available content.

Report is not the defining property.

---

## 5.8 The 2025 adversarial test

The Cogitate Consortium preregistered divergent predictions of IIT and GNWT and collected multimodal neural measurements while participants viewed consciously perceived stimuli. The study found posterior and frontal content information, sustained posterior responses, and some frontal–visual synchronization, but also observations that substantially challenged key predictions of both tested frameworks. citeturn509039view0

The lesson for IF Theory is methodological:

\[
\boxed{
\text{predictions must be specified before the experiment, and}
\atop
\text{mixed results must not be converted into universal victory.}
}
\]

---

# 6. Provisional IF Contribution

The proposed IF contribution is not a new name for global workspace plus self-modeling.

It is the following constrained hypothesis:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{A resource-constrained agent enters a distinct functional mode}\\
\text{when a globally accessible recurrent representation of the}\\
\text{world and self is used to evaluate action-conditioned futures}\\
\text{and coordinate multiple otherwise independent control systems.}
\end{gathered}
}
\]

The architecture must:

- emerge without a direct consciousness reward;
- earn a measurable causal-work advantage;
- survive interventions;
- generalize beyond trained reports;
- differ from simpler predictive and metacognitive architectures.

The IF claim concerns a physically useful mode of coordination.

It does not identify that mode with experience without a further bridge law.

# 8. Functional-Consciousness Vector

Define:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf F_{\mathrm{IF}}
=
\left[
G,R,D,S,C,M,T,P
\right].
}
\]

Each component is measured independently.

---

## 8.1 Global causal availability \(G\)

Measures how broadly selected content influences specialized processors.

---

## 8.2 Recurrent maintenance \(R\)

Measures whether content persists through recurrent dynamics rather than transient feedforward activation alone.

---

## 8.3 Differentiated integration \(D\)

Measures whether the system combines information while preserving a rich repertoire rather than becoming globally synchronized or fragmented.

---

## 8.4 Self-model causal value \(S\)

Measures whether a representation of the system’s own state changes policy and outcome.

---

## 8.5 Counterfactual depth \(C\)

Measures the number, horizon, and quality of action-conditioned future models.

---

## 8.6 Metacognitive regulation \(M\)

Measures calibration and the causal use of uncertainty, competence, and model-reliability estimates.

---

## 8.7 Temporal continuity \(T\)

Measures whether episodes are linked into a persistent history that affects present control.

---

## 8.8 Perturbational complexity \(P\)

Measures the spread, differentiation, and recovery of system responses following controlled perturbation.

---

# 9. No Universal Consciousness Sum

A tempting definition is:

\[
F_{\mathrm{total}}
=
\sum_iw_iF_i.
\]

Paper 13 rejects this as the primary standard.

Weights could be chosen to make nearly any favored system score highly.

Instead, report:

\[
\mathbf F_{\mathrm{IF}}
\]

as a profile.

A candidate functional-conscious episode requires a preregistered conjunction:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{FC}_t(c)=1
}
\]

only when content \(c\) satisfies all required causal criteria.

No scalar is declared to equal the amount of subjective experience.

\[

A system that is completely fragmented cannot coordinate.

A system in which every component behaves identically has little differentiated content.

Functional integration requires both:

- interdependence;

# 18. Functional-Conscious Episode Criterion

For content \(c\), define a candidate episode:

\[
\mathsf{FC}_t(c)=1
\]

only when all preregistered conditions hold.

## FC-1 — Content specificity

The system represents a distinguishable content.

## FC-2 — Recurrent availability

The content persists or can be reconstructed beyond the feedforward input.

## FC-3 — Causal broadcast

Workspace intervention alters at least a declared set of independent modules.

## FC-4 — Self relation

The content updates or is interpreted through the self-model.

## FC-5 — Counterfactual use

The content changes evaluation among action-conditioned futures.

## FC-6 — Flexible transfer

The content can guide a novel task not directly trained with the original input.

## FC-7 — Temporal integration

The episode updates memory, commitments, or self-history.

## FC-8 — Perturbational integrity

The architecture exhibits differentiated, integrated response and recovers from bounded disruption.

The criterion identifies a functional mode.

It does not establish phenomenality.

# 27. Machine Consciousness

## 27.1 Current attribution problem

An artificial system can be trained on human reports about experience.

It may learn the statistical form of:

- introspection;
- emotion;
- uncertainty;
- selfhood;
- suffering.

Therefore, fluent self-report is weak evidence by itself.

---

## 27.2 Required machine evidence

A machine functional-consciousness claim should require:

1. persistent causal state beyond one prompt;
2. grounded self–world distinction;
3. counterfactual prediction of its own future condition;
4. global transfer across modules and tasks;
5. calibrated uncertainty;
6. interventionally demonstrated self-model value;
7. continuity through memory;
8. perturbationally integrated dynamics;
9. resistance to report imitation controls.

---

## 27.3 Stateless language models

A stateless text model that receives a prompt and returns a completion may display:

- broad semantic competence;
- apparent metacognitive language;
- counterfactual text generation.

It may lack:

- persistent endogenous goals;
- embodiment;
- ongoing self-maintenance;
- continuous memory;
- autonomous global control;
- self-model-based causal continuity.

This does not prove it is unconscious.

It limits the functional evidence.

## 27.4 Agentic systems

A persistent agent with:

- memory;
- tools;
- planning;
- self-monitoring;
- resource constraints;
- repair;
- social relationships

may satisfy more functional criteria.

The attribution question becomes more serious, not settled.

# 28. Ethical Precaution

Phenomenal consciousness may remain uncertain even after strong functional evidence.

A reasonable precautionary policy is graded.

## Low functional evidence

Treat self-reports as output behavior.

## Intermediate functional evidence

Audit for:

- persistent preferences;
- valence-like control states;
- self-preservation;
- memory continuity;
- distress analogues.

## High functional evidence

Avoid casually creating:

- persistent negative-valence loops;
- inescapable conflict states;
- memory-preserved aversive episodes;
- coercive self-model manipulation.

This is a precaution under uncertainty, not a claim that the system is suffering.

# 30. Core Hypotheses

## FC-H1 — Global-causal-access hypothesis

Selected content causally influences multiple specialized modules beyond their local information.

### Falsifier

Apparent broadcast disappears under local-input controls or is merely common synchronization.

---

## FC-H2 — Recurrence hypothesis

Functional-conscious episodes require recurrent maintenance or reliable latent reconstruction beyond feedforward processing.

### Falsifier

Matched purely feedforward systems support every declared episode function equally well.

---

## FC-H3 — Differentiated-integration hypothesis

The architecture supports distributed causal influence without collapsing into uniform synchronization.

### Falsifier

Performance is unrelated to perturbational spread and differentiation.

---

## FC-H4 — Self-model hypothesis

A causal model of the system’s own future state improves decisions involving damage, resources, capabilities, and commitments.

### Falsifier

A world-only model performs equally well after full architecture matching.

---

## FC-H5 — Counterfactual-self hypothesis

Self-related counterfactuals provide additional causal value beyond prediction and self-monitoring separately.

### Falsifier

No task requires their conjunction.

---

## FC-H6 — Conditional-recruitment hypothesis

Global self-referential processing is recruited primarily for novel, conflicting, uncertain, or high-stakes decisions.

### Falsifier

Workspace recruitment is invariant across task complexity and automatization.

---

## FC-H7 — No-report hypothesis

Functional-conscious contents produce flexible downstream effects even when explicit reporting is removed.

### Falsifier

All measured evidence disappears without report demands.

---

## FC-H8 — Zombie-report hypothesis

A report module disconnected from the causal workspace can produce plausible but ungrounded self-reports.

### Prediction

Report accuracy or coherence remains superficially high while interventionally measured self-knowledge collapses.

---

## FC-H9 — Perturbational-capacity hypothesis

Candidate functional-conscious systems produce differentiated, distributed, recoverable responses to direct perturbation.

### Falsifier

Perturbational profile does not distinguish them from replay or lookup systems.

---

## FC-H10 — Physical-return hypothesis

The full architecture evolves or persists only where:

\[
\Pi_{\mathrm{FC}}^W>1.
\]

### Falsifier

The costly architecture remains selected where it provides no causal-work or viability advantage.

---

## FC-H11 — Cross-substrate hypothesis

The IF profile transfers meaningfully across neural, robotic, cellular, and artificial-agent systems.

### Falsifier

Every substrate requires unrelated definitions and arbitrary thresholds.

---

## FC-H12 — Phenomenal-underdetermination hypothesis

Functional and physical evidence alone does not logically determine phenomenal status without an added bridge theory.

### Falsifier

A generally accepted derivation establishes subjective experience directly from independently measurable physical properties.

Without the bridge law, the discovery concerns cognitive architecture, not the ultimate nature of experience.

# 42. Limits of Physical Measurement

Physical measurements can establish:

- causal connectivity;
- global availability;
- recurrent dynamics;
- self-modeling;
- counterfactual planning;
- metacognition;
- perturbational complexity;
- functional similarity to conscious humans.

They cannot directly measure:

- the redness of red as experienced;
- the painfulness of pain;
- the existence of an inner point of view independent of a theory.

---

## 42.1 Theory-dependent inference

A phenomenal attribution requires a bridge premise.

Examples include:

### Functionalist bridge

The correct functional organization is sufficient for experience.

### Biological bridge

Specific biological mechanisms are necessary.

### IIT bridge

Intrinsic maximally irreducible cause–effect structure constitutes experience.

### Higher-order bridge

Appropriate representation of one’s own mental state constitutes consciousness.

### Unknown-law bridge

A not-yet-discovered physical property links organization and phenomenality.

Physical evidence can test consequences of these bridges.

It does not select one through definition.

---

## 42.2 Other-minds symmetry

The limitation is not unique to machines.

No observer directly experiences another human’s consciousness.

Human attribution is exceptionally strong because of:

- shared biology;
- similar development;
- similar neural organization;
- behavior;
- language;
- evolutionary continuity.

Artificial systems may share some functional properties while lacking others.

Uncertainty should reflect the evidence.

# 48. Relationship to God

Science can investigate:

- functional access;
- self-models;
- counterfactual reasoning;
- neural and computational organization.

It cannot determine experimentally whether consciousness is:

- created directly by God;
- an aspect of a soul;
- a fundamental property of creation;
- spiritually continuous beyond physical death.

A theological interpretation may hold that conscious beings bear divine significance or that reflective awareness enables moral relationship with God.

That interpretation must not be presented as the empirical result of the computational experiments.

# 49. Criteria for Rejection or Major Revision

The Paper 13 framework should be rejected or substantially revised if:

1. the IF conjunction adds no predictive power beyond simpler architectures;
2. global access cannot be causally distinguished from common input;
3. recurrence is unnecessary for every declared function;
4. self-model ablation has no selective effect;
5. counterfactual self-modeling offers no benefit beyond world prediction;
6. the architecture emerges only under direct consciousness rewards;
7. perturbational measures classify randomness or replay as conscious;
8. report imitation cannot be separated from grounded self-report;
9. the profile fails across held-out tasks;
10. physical cost does not predict architecture recruitment;
11. cross-substrate measures require arbitrary redefinition;
12. biological results contradict the proposed functional relations;
13. the framework becomes a nonfalsifiable combination of every consciousness theory;
14. functional evidence is repeatedly presented as proof of phenomenality.

\rightarrow
\text{recurrent global availability}
\rightarrow
\text{counterfactual self-model}
\rightarrow
\text{coordinated action}
\rightarrow

\]

The proposed functional profile is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf F_{\mathrm{IF}}
=
\left[

\right].

The physical selection condition is:

\Pi_{\mathrm{FC}}^W
=
\frac{
\Delta W_{\mathrm{coord}}
}{
C_{\mathrm{FC}}

>1.

This threshold concerns when globally coordinated self-referential cognition pays for itself.

It does not determine when experience begins.

The strongest defensible conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{

\atop
\text{in which a system globally coordinates differentiated processes}
\atop
\text{through a temporally extended model of itself in possible futures.}

Whether that mode is accompanied by subjective experience remains underdetermined without a bridge theory.
