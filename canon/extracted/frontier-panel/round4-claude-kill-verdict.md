K4 — Is a negative paper the right output, and what's the honest title?

Yes. Unequivocally. And it's a better paper than the positive one would have been, because the field is drowning in unfalsifiable agency-thermodynamics frameworks and almost none of their authors ran the experiment that could kill their own central claim. You did, three times, and it died. That is a real contribution and it is rarer than a positive result. Publish it.

But do not write "we tried to find an invariant and failed" — that's a lab notebook, not a paper. The paper's spine is the mechanism of the failure, which is general and predictive. The strongest honest framing:

Title: No Substrate-Independent Invariant for Causal-Work Agency: Estimator Non-Portability of Used-Information Across Predictive, Filtering, and Gradient-Climbing Agents.

(Or tighter: "Used Information" Is Not Substrate-Portable: A Pre-Registered Falsification of Causal-Work Universality.)

Central claim (the thing that makes it cited rather than filed): Any attempt to normalize interventionally-enabled work by an information quantity fails across agent families for a structural reason, not a numerical one: "information the agent uses" is only definable relative to an assumed internal representational format (predictive error, control–drift MI, control–work MI), and no single format survives all three of a Kalman predictor, a ring-drift estimator, and a scalar gradient-climber. Gradient-climbing agents perform causal work while carrying no recoverable predictive information at all, falsifying the implicit representational assumption shared by semantic-information accounts of agency.

That last sentence is your Kolchinsky–Wolpert contact point and it's the most valuable thing you own now. Lead the abstract with the chemotaxis counterexample, not the σ tables. The tables are evidence; the counterexample is the idea.
