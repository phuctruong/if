# IF Theory  
## The Game of Life, Recharged

> An experimental computational framework for studying informational charge, entropy, expansion, memory, reflection, and self-repair in artificial universes.

---

## Overview

**IF Theory**—Informational Field / Informational Battery Theory—is a computational research project that extends Conway-style cellular automata into expandable toy universes.

Each universe begins with simple local rules. From those rules, we investigate whether larger phenomena can emerge:

- structure,
- entropy,
- expansion,
- persistent memory,
- self-repair,
- computation,
- replication,
- cooperation,
- parasitism,
- and reflective behavior.

The central question is:

> **What if Conway’s Game of Life began as a Big Bang and evolved structures that learned to recharge themselves?**

IF Theory does not claim to reproduce the physical universe or prove a new theory of cosmology. It provides a programmable environment in which informational-cosmology ideas can be expressed precisely, simulated, measured, challenged, and potentially falsified.

---

## The Core Idea

Conway’s Game of Life demonstrates that surprisingly complex structures can emerge from extremely simple rules.

IF Theory asks what happens when a cellular universe also contains:

1. **Informational charge**  
   A cell’s capacity to perform work, preserve organization, or influence its neighborhood.

2. **Entropy**  
   Local disorder, uncertainty, incompatibility, or loss of usable informational structure.

3. **Phase**  
   A cell’s internal orientation or state relative to nearby cells.

4. **Memory**  
   Compressed information about a cell’s previous states and interactions.

5. **Reflection**  
   The ability of a stable structure to use memory to alter future behavior.

6. **Expansion**  
   The ability of the simulated universe to create new space when activity reaches its boundary.

The purpose is not to install complexity manually. The purpose is to discover the smallest rules under which complex behavior appears spontaneously.

---

## Scientific Status

IF Theory is currently a **toy computational framework** and an exploratory research program.

It may be useful for studying questions such as:

- Can expansion change the lifetime of organized structures?
- Can local memory improve persistence?
- Can reflective behavior emerge from stability?
- Can self-repairing structures evolve?
- Under what conditions does cooperation outperform extraction?
- Are there critical transitions between dead, frozen, chaotic, living, and reflective universes?
- Can persistent IF structures perform universal computation?
- Which quantities, if any, remain invariant as the board expands?

The project distinguishes three levels of claim.

### Supported computational claim

> Particular local rules produce particular measurable behaviors in the simulator.

### Interpretive claim

> Those behaviors may provide useful analogies for entropy, emergence, biological organization, cognition, or cosmology.

### Unsupported claim

> The simulator proves that the real universe follows the same rules.

The third claim is explicitly outside the scope of the project.

## Design Principles

### 1. Minimal rules first

The initial model should use as few primitive variables and update equations as possible.

Complexity should be earned through emergence, not inserted through elaborate rules.

### 2. Measurement before interpretation

Every important concept must correspond to a computable quantity.

Terms such as *charge*, *entropy*, *reflection*, *life*, and *recharge* require operational definitions.

### 3. Search rule space

No single rule set should be treated as sacred.

The project will simulate thousands of parameter combinations and classify their outcomes.

### 4. Reproducibility

Every published figure should be reproducible from:

- a configuration file,
- a random seed,
- a versioned simulator,
- and a Jupyter notebook.

### 5. Scientific humility

The simulator should reveal where the theory fails as clearly as where it succeeds.

### 6. Moral behavior must emerge

Cooperation, care, and MaxLove will not initially be hard-coded as universal laws.

Instead, later experiments will test whether agency-preserving cooperation becomes more stable, productive, or resilient than parasitism under specified conditions.

## The IF Cell

The initial IF cell contains four primary state variables:

\[
x_i(t) = \left(q_i,e_i,\phi_i,r_i\right)
\]

where:

- \(q_i\) = informational charge,
- \(e_i\) = local entropy,
- \(\phi_i\) = phase or orientation,
- \(r_i\) = reflection capacity.

Additional derived variables may include:

- activity,
- local alignment,
- stability,
- age,
- memory depth,
- boundary pressure,
- neighborhood complexity,
- and resource flow.

The first implementation should keep all primary variables bounded:

\[
q_i,e_i,r_i \in [0,1]
\]

and phase may be represented as:

\[
\phi_i \in [0,2\pi)
\]

or, in the simplest discrete model:

\[
\phi_i \in \{-1,+1\}
\]

The discrete version should be implemented first because it is easier to inspect and falsify.

## IF Life v0.1

For each cell \(i\), define local phase alignment:

\[
A_i(t)=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}_i|}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_i}
\frac{1+\cos\left(\phi_j-\phi_i\right)}{2}
\]

so that:

\[
0\leq A_i\leq1
\]

A provisional update system is:

\[
q_i(t+1)=
\operatorname{clip}
\left[
q_i(t)
+\alpha A_i(t)
-\beta e_i(t)
-\mu a_i(t),
0,1
\right]
\]

\[
e_i(t+1)=
\operatorname{clip}
\left[
e_i(t)
+\gamma\left(1-A_i(t)\right)
-\delta r_i(t)
+\xi_i(t),
0,1
\right]
\]

\[
r_i(t+1)=
\operatorname{clip}
\left[
r_i(t)
+\eta\,\sigma_i(t)
-\kappa r_i(t),
0,1
\right]
\]

where:

- \(\alpha\) controls charge gained through alignment,
- \(\beta\) controls charge lost through entropy,
- \(\mu\) is the cost of activity,
- \(\gamma\) controls entropy generated by misalignment,
- \(\delta\) controls entropy reduction associated with reflection,
- \(\xi_i\) represents noise or perturbation,
- \(\eta\) controls reflection gained through stability,
- \(\kappa\) represents memory or reflection decay,
- \(\sigma_i\) is a measurable local stability score.

A cell is provisionally active when:

\[
q_i(t)-e_i(t)>\theta_A
\]

These equations are hypotheses, not axioms. They exist to provide a minimal executable starting point.

## Preventing Free Recharge

A major danger is accidentally creating charge from nothing.

Therefore, later versions should distinguish between:

- **stored charge**,
- **transferred charge**,
- **usable free energy**,
- **waste entropy**,
- and **external or boundary input**.

Reflection should not simply delete entropy without cost.

A more physically disciplined model may require:

\[
\Delta E_{\text{local}}<0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\Delta E_{\text{environment}}>0
\]

Reflection would then reorganize or export entropy rather than annihilate it.

The project will compare:

1. nonconservative exploratory rules,
2. globally conservative rules,
3. open universes with external input,
4. and expanding universes whose new cells contain specified initial resources.

## The Expandable Universe

Classic cellular automata normally operate on a fixed or periodic board.

IF Theory allows the domain to expand.

Let boundary pressure be:

\[
B(t)=
\frac{1}{|\partial\Omega_t|}
\sum_{i\in\partial\Omega_t}a_i(t)
\]

where \(\partial\Omega_t\) is the current boundary.

When:

\[
B(t)>\theta_B
\]

for a sustained number of steps, the simulator adds a new region around the current grid:

\[
\Omega_t\rightarrow\Omega_{t+1}
\]

Several expansion laws will be compared:

- threshold expansion,
- constant-rate expansion,
- activity-proportional expansion,
- charge-funded expansion,
- stochastic expansion,
- and locally expanding space.

Expansion must have an explicit accounting rule. New space cannot silently introduce unlimited energy unless that is the declared experimental condition.

## The Big Bang Seed

The first cosmological experiment begins with a highly symmetric, highly charged state:

\[
q_i(0)=1
\]

\[
e_i(0)=0
\]

\[
\phi_i(0)=\phi_0
\]

A small perturbation breaks the symmetry:

\[
\phi_k(0)=\phi_0+\epsilon
\]

or:

\[
e_k(0)=\epsilon
\]

The simulation then measures whether the perturbation:

- disappears,
- propagates,
- fragments the universe,
- generates persistent structures,
- triggers expansion,
- creates oscillations,
- or produces higher-order organization.

This is called a **Big Bang seed** as a computational metaphor. It is not presented as a literal model of the physical Big Bang.

## Global Metrics

The first simulator will track at least the following quantities.

### Total charge

\[
Q(t)=\sum_i q_i(t)
\]

### Total entropy

\[
E(t)=\sum_i e_i(t)
\]

### Total reflection

\[
R(t)=\sum_i r_i(t)
\]

### Active population

\[
P(t)=\sum_i \mathbf{1}\left[q_i(t)-e_i(t)>\theta_A\right]
\]

### Mean alignment

\[
\bar{A}(t)=\frac{1}{N_t}\sum_i A_i(t)
\]

### Occupied area

\[
V(t)=|\Omega_t|
\]

### Expansion rate

\[
H_{\mathrm{IF}}(t)=
\frac{V(t+1)-V(t)}{V(t)}
\]

This is an internal simulation metric and is not equivalent to the cosmological Hubble parameter.

### Structural persistence

Track connected structures across time and measure:

- lifetime,
- size,
- shape retention,
- movement,
- replication,
- and recovery after perturbation.

### Still-water score

A provisional composite measure is:

\[
S(t)=
w_Q\tilde{Q}(t)
-w_E\tilde{E}(t)
+w_R\tilde{R}(t)
+w_P\tilde{P}(t)
\]

where each term is normalized.

Because arbitrary composite scores can conceal bad assumptions, all component metrics must also be reported separately.

## Universe Classification

Each simulation will be classified into one or more regimes.

### Dead universe

Activity falls below a minimum threshold and does not recover.

### Frozen universe

The universe reaches a nearly static configuration with little novelty.

### Chaotic universe

Local states remain highly unstable and persistent structures are rare.

### Explosive universe

Activity or expansion grows without a meaningful bound.

### Oscillatory universe

The system repeatedly cycles through recognizable macrostates.

### Living universe

Persistent structures maintain boundaries, process resources, or reproduce.

### Reflective universe

Some structures use accumulated state information to alter their behavior and improve persistence or repair.

### Cooperative universe

Resource-sharing structures outperform isolated or purely extractive structures under a specified fitness measure.

These names describe computational regimes, not biological or metaphysical proof.

## Falsifiable Questions

The project becomes scientifically useful only when it can fail.

Initial falsifiable hypotheses include:

### H1

Expandable grids produce longer-lived structures than fixed grids under at least some resource-conserving rule families.

### H2

Reflection improves recovery from perturbation only when its informational benefit exceeds its energetic cost.

## Philosophical Motivation

IF Theory begins with an intuition:

> A universe may be understood not only by what matter it contains, but by how usable organization is stored, discharged, transferred, remembered, and restored.

A highly ordered initial state resembles a charged informational battery. Symmetry breaking creates differences. Differences enable interaction. Interaction creates history. History enables memory. Memory may eventually enable reflection.

The strongest philosophical possibility is not that intelligence defeats entropy without cost. It is that intelligence becomes increasingly capable of preserving meaningful organization while paying, redirecting, and accounting for that cost.

Within later IF experiments, **MaxLove** will refer to a proposed strategy class:

> Preserve and increase agency by helping systems recharge without unnecessarily destroying the agency of other systems.

Whether that strategy succeeds is an experimental question inside the model, not a predetermined conclusion.

- a clean simulator,
- reproducible experiments,

## How novel is it really?

### 1. The universe as computation is not novel

The idea that the physical universe could be a cellular automaton or discrete computational system goes back through Konrad Zuse, Edward Fredkin, Stephen Wolfram and, in a more technically developed form, Gerard ’t Hooft’s cellular-automaton interpretation of quantum mechanics. The core proposition—simple local rules generating physical reality—is therefore established territory. citeturn606853search14turn606853search15turn606853search26

### 2. Information as physically fundamental is not novel

Wheeler’s “it from bit,” the holographic principle, quantum-information reconstructions of physics, and constructor theory all give information a foundational physical role. The holographic principle in particular connects information bounds with spacetime geometry, while constructor theory attempts to formulate physical laws in terms of possible and impossible information transformations. citeturn820329search6turn891768academia38turn891768academia37

### 3. Order emerging through entropy production is not novel

Prigogine’s theory of dissipative structures established that organized, stable structures can arise in open systems far from thermodynamic equilibrium. This is important because life does not maintain order by reversing the second law; it maintains local organization through energy flows while producing entropy in its environment. Prigogine received the 1977 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for developing the actual nonequilibrium thermodynamic theory behind this phenomenon. citeturn356282search4turn356282search9

### 4. Information processing having a thermodynamic cost is not novel

Landauer’s principle connects logically irreversible information loss to thermodynamic entropy production. This means that memory, reflection, error correction, and computation cannot simply remove entropy for free. Any rigorous version of IF recharge must account for where energy comes from, where waste goes, and which correlations are preserved or destroyed. citeturn891768academia36turn891768academia39

### 5. Self-maintaining systems as inference is not novel

Friston’s free-energy principle describes self-organizing systems in terms of maintaining characteristic states and can be interpreted through active inference, autopoiesis, and self-evidencing. IF Theory’s sequence of stability → memory → reflection → adaptive preservation therefore occupies territory already addressed by sophisticated mathematical frameworks. citeturn611737academia38

### 6. Information-based consciousness is not novel

Integrated Information Theory links consciousness to intrinsic cause-effect structure and integrated information. Global workspace theories link consciousness to widespread information availability or broadcasting. Active-inference approaches also attempt to relate self-modeling and inference to sentience. ## 2. “Entropy” risks meaning four incompatible things

IF Theory currently moves between:

- thermodynamic entropy,
- Shannon information entropy,
- algorithmic complexity,

Calling all three “entropy” would let the theory explain almost anything after the fact.

This may be the single most dangerous conceptual weakness.

## 3. Recharge may be disguised perpetual motion

If reflection directly decreases entropy without:

- consuming free energy,
- increasing entropy elsewhere,
- exporting waste,

A thermostat uses past and present information to alter future behavior. Error-correcting code preserves states. Neural cellular automata can repair damaged patterns. None of these facts demonstrates subjective experience.

# 1. Introduction

Simple local systems can produce unexpectedly rich macroscopic behavior. Cellular automata demonstrate that deterministic local transitions can generate persistent objects, movement, replication, and computation. More recent neural cellular-automaton systems have demonstrated learned growth and regeneration from decentralized local interactions. These results establish that complex-looking organization need not be explicitly controlled at the global level, but they do not establish that the physical universe is literally a cellular automaton. citeturn252506search1turn252506search2

Nonequilibrium thermodynamics likewise demonstrates that entropy-producing systems can generate and sustain ordered structures when supplied by appropriate flows of matter or energy. Prigogine’s work on dissipative structures showed that irreversible processes far from equilibrium can support organized states rather than producing only featureless decay. Such organization does not violate the second law: local order is maintained through exchanges with a larger environment and accompanying entropy production. citeturn985462search1turn985462search5

Information is also physically embodied. Landauer argued that logically irreversible computation is associated with physical dissipation, and later experiments demonstrated the expected thermodynamic bound in controlled one-bit systems. This does not mean that every bit has a fixed energy or that abstract information is itself a free source of power. It means that physical operations involving information have implementation-dependent energetic and entropic consequences. citeturn262952search0turn262952search1

Research has further attempted to distinguish information that merely correlates with an environment from information that contributes to a system’s continued existence. Kolchinsky and Wolpert developed an intervention-based definition of semantic information for autonomous nonequilibrium systems: correlations count as meaningful to the system when removing them damages its viability. Causal-emergence research has separately shown that, under specified causal measures, a macroscopic description can sometimes possess greater effective causal organization than a microscopic description. citeturn866367search0turn866367search1

IF Theory enters this existing landscape. Its foundational ingredients are therefore not individually new. Its possible contribution is a specific **cross-scale research program**:

\[
\boxed{
\text{nonequilibrium capacity}
\rightarrow
\text{persistent organization}
\rightarrow
\text{self-maintenance}
\rightarrow
\text{predictive agency}
\rightarrow
\text{reflection}
\rightarrow
\text{cooperation}
}
\]

The program asks whether these transitions can be measured under one consistent resource-and-information accounting system, without inserting life, agency, consciousness, morality, or cosmic purpose into the primitive rules.

A speculative second branch asks whether a related informational state could constrain emergent geometry, apparent missing gravity, and cosmic expansion. That branch begins with a much higher evidential burden. The standard cosmological model remains highly successful across the final Planck temperature, polarization, and lensing measurements, while DESI DR2 has provided precise baryon-acoustic-oscillation measurements from more than fourteen million galaxies and quasars. Any IF cosmology must reproduce the successful predictions of existing cosmology before claiming to replace its dark sector. citeturn866367academia51turn866367search3

This paper establishes the rules under which those questions may be investigated.

The central scientific question of IF Theory is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Can one explicit physical and informational accounting framework}
\atop
\text{connect the emergence of structure, life, agency, reflection,}
\atop
\text{and cooperation without placing those outcomes in the primitive rules?}
}
\]

A secondary, substantially more speculative question is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Can any successful IF law be extended to make unique quantitative}
\atop
\text{predictions about spacetime, gravity, cosmic growth, or expansion?}
}
\]

The first question can be studied through artificial universes, deterministic simulations, causal interventions, and potentially laboratory systems.

The second requires contact with established fundamental physics and observational data. It cannot be answered by visual resemblance between an expanding simulation and the observed universe.

# 4. Prior Research and the Novelty Boundary

## 4.1 Cellular automata and computational universes

Conway-style cellular automata establish that small local rule sets can produce complex persistent patterns. Computational interpretations of physics go substantially further, proposing that deterministic discrete substrates might underlie or reproduce aspects of quantum theory. Gerard ’t Hooft’s cellular-automaton interpretation is one developed example, including attempts to relate deterministic ontological states to quantum descriptions. citeturn252506search1turn252506academia34

IF Theory therefore cannot claim novelty merely for proposing that the universe may be computational, discrete, graph-based, or locally updated.

Its stricter requirement is to demonstrate that a minimal substrate produces specific cross-scale transitions under explicit thermodynamic and intervention-based accounting.

---

## 4.2 Nonequilibrium order and dissipative structures

The proposition that order can emerge in entropy-producing open systems is established in nonequilibrium thermodynamics. Dissipative structures remain organized through continued exchanges with their environments, and biological nonequilibrium physics studies how living systems maintain organization through driven processes. citeturn985462search1turn262952search6

IF Theory therefore cannot claim novelty for stating that life preserves local order while exporting entropy.

Its possible contribution is a quantitative account of when persistent organization begins using internally maintained predictive information to alter its own resource access and viability.

---

## 4.3 Information thermodynamics

Landauer’s principle and subsequent theoretical and experimental work establish a relationship between logically irreversible information processing and thermodynamic cost. Information-bearing physical systems must obey ordinary physical laws; computation does not grant exemption from energy conservation or entropy production. citeturn262952search0turn262952search1turn262952search5

IF Theory therefore rejects any rule in which reflection, intelligence, or observation freely removes entropy.

The complete cost of sensing, storage, error correction, communication, prediction, action selection, and erasure must be included in any claim that information creates an adaptive advantage.

---

## 4.4 Nonequilibrium free energy and relative entropy

In defined isothermal stochastic settings, the departure of a probability distribution from equilibrium can be related to a free-energy difference through relative entropy. This relationship provides a mathematically disciplined starting point for the informational-battery concept, but it does not justify applying one formula universally across arbitrary cosmological, biological, and computational systems. citeturn262952academia35turn262952academia37

IF Theory therefore treats nonequilibrium free energy as one possible implementation of physical battery capacity, not as a final universal definition.

---

## 4.5 Semantic information and autonomous agency

Intervention-based semantic-information frameworks already ask which correlations matter to an autonomous system’s viability. This is a direct precedent for IF Theory’s attempt to distinguish irrelevant stored information from information that causally supports continued organization. citeturn866367search0

The potentially new IF question is whether semantic information has a measurable **net causal-work value** after the full physical cost of maintaining and using the information is deducted—and whether a common dimensionless threshold predicts agency across different substrates.

---

## 4.6 Causal emergence

Causal-emergence frameworks quantify cases in which an appropriately selected macrolevel possesses stronger effective causal structure than the underlying microlevel. This provides a possible method for identifying organisms or agents without assuming that the programmer’s preferred spatial grouping is the correct one. citeturn866367search1turn866367search6

IF Theory therefore cannot claim that “the whole becomes more than the parts” as an original discovery.

It must identify precise coarse-grainings, interventions, and held-out predictions under which a detected macrostructure has explanatory or causal advantages.

---

## 4.7 Active inference and the free-energy principle

The free-energy principle and active inference provide formal accounts of self-organization, inference, action, and systems that appear to maintain characteristic states. Some formulations explicitly connect counterfactual beliefs, future planning, and self-evidencing behavior. citeturn985462academia25turn985462academia26

IF Theory therefore cannot claim novelty merely for describing organisms as systems that infer environmental conditions or preserve viable states.

A successful IF theory must show what its causal-work measure predicts that active-inference or viability-based frameworks do not, and it must compare the frameworks directly.

---

## 4.8 Consciousness theories

Integrated information theory, global neuronal workspace theory, recurrent-processing theories, and other approaches already propose relationships between information processing and consciousness. A large preregistered adversarial collaboration published in 2025 directly compared key predictions of integrated-information and global-workspace theories, finding a mixed pattern that challenged important elements of both. The result illustrates that broad compatibility with neural data is insufficient; theories need predictions that differ from those of their competitors before the experiment is conducted. citeturn866367search2

IF Theory therefore does not presently claim to explain subjective experience.

Its initial consciousness-related objective is restricted to the emergence and causal necessity of particular **functional capabilities**, including global information sharing, counterfactual modeling, self-representation, policy competition, and policy revision.

---

## 4.9 Cosmology and the dark sector

The final Planck analysis found strong consistency with the spatially flat six-parameter \(\Lambda\)CDM model and separately constrained baryonic and cold-dark-matter densities. DESI DR2 has sharpened measurements of the cosmic expansion history; combined inferences concerning evolving dark energy remain sensitive to model parametrization and companion datasets. citeturn866367academia51turn866367search3turn866367academia49

IF Theory therefore cannot reject dark matter or dark energy simply because an information-based alternative is philosophically attractive.

A credible IF alternative must reproduce or improve upon:

- cosmic microwave background temperature and polarization spectra;
- CMB gravitational lensing;
- baryon acoustic oscillations;
- primordial element constraints;
- galactic and cluster dynamics;
- gravitational lensing;
- large-scale structure growth;
- weak lensing;
- cluster mergers;
- gravitational-wave propagation;
- and the measured expansion history.

---

## 4.10 Provisional novelty claim

The individual components of IF Theory are not novel.

The potentially novel contribution is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{A single intervention-based accounting framework tracks how}\\
\text{nonequilibrium capacity becomes persistent organization,}\\
\text{how organization acquires causally useful predictive information,}\\
\text{and when that information becomes net-positive physical agency.}
\end{gathered}
}
\]

A stronger, unproven extension asks whether the same underlying state could constrain both emergent gravitational response and cosmic expansion.

This novelty claim remains provisional until a comprehensive literature review and working mathematical model demonstrate that the exact formulation has not previously been published.

# 5. Formal Substrate

## 5.1 Universe state

The minimal IF artificial universe is represented as:

\[
\mathcal U_t=
\left(
G_t,X_t,\varepsilon_t,\Lambda_t
\right),
\]

where:

- \(G_t=(V_t,E_t)\) is a directed or undirected causal-interaction graph;
- \(X_t=\{x_i(t)\}\) is the collection of local internal states;
- \(\varepsilon_t=\{\varepsilon_i(t)\}\) is the local physical-resource distribution;
- \(\Lambda_t\) specifies environmental boundary conditions or external reservoirs.

The dynamics are described by a transition law:

\[
P_\theta
\left(
\mathcal U_{t+1}\mid \mathcal U_t
\right),
\]

or, for a deterministic model:

\[
\mathcal U_{t+1}
=
T_\theta(\mathcal U_t).
\]

The primitive rules may include:

- state transitions;
- local transport;
- interaction costs;
- noise;
- resource conversion;
- node or edge creation;
- node or edge destruction.

The primitive rules may not contain variables whose names or functions directly encode:

- life;
- organisms;
- intelligence;
- agency;
- reflection;
- consciousness;
- morality;
- cooperation bonuses;
- love;
- divinity;
- cosmic purpose.

These may appear only as higher-level classifications supported by operational tests.

---

## 5.2 Locality

An update is locally governed when the next state of an element depends only on a formally specified causal neighborhood:

\[
x_i(t+1)
=
T_i
\left[
x_i(t),
\{x_j(t):j\in\mathcal N_i(t)\},
\varepsilon_i(t)
\right].
\]

The neighborhood may change through lawful graph dynamics, but it may not be retrospectively altered to produce a desired outcome.

---

## 5.3 Open and closed systems

Each experiment must state whether the simulated universe is:

- energetically closed;
- open to an external resource reservoir;
- open to matter exchange;
- driven by boundary conditions;
- or expanding through a defined resource conversion.

No claim of self-recharge is valid unless the source and destination of every relevant energy and entropy flow are included.

# 6. The Three-Ledger Requirement

IF Theory requires three separate ledgers.

## 6.1 Energy ledger

For a designated system:

\[
\Delta U
=
Q_{\mathrm{in}}
-
Q_{\mathrm{out}}
+
W_{\mathrm{in}}
-
W_{\mathrm{out}}
+
\Delta U_{\mathrm{created\ or\ removed}},
\]

where the final term must be zero unless the model explicitly includes a lawful conversion with another accounted field.

Every implementation must test its energy balance numerically.

---

## 6.2 Thermodynamic-entropy ledger

For a system plus its environment:

\[
\Delta S_{\mathrm{total}}
=
\Delta S_{\mathrm{system}}
+
\Delta S_{\mathrm{environment}}
\geq 0
\]

for processes to which the ordinary second-law framework applies.

Local entropy reduction is permitted only when compensated by entropy production, heat flow, resource consumption, or another explicitly modeled process.

## 6.3 Information ledger

Relevant information measures may include:

### Shannon entropy

\[
H(X)
=
-\sum_x p(x)\log p(x).

### Mutual information

\[
I(X;Y)
=
\sum_{x,y}
p(x,y)
\log
\frac{p(x,y)}

\]

### Conditional mutual information

\[
I(X;Y\mid Z).
\]

### Relative entropy

\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\parallel q)

\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}.

### Algorithmic complexity

\[

under an explicitly stated approximation or compression method.

### Effective or causal information

A specified intervention-based quantity measuring how selectively states cause later states.

These quantities are not interchangeable.

No paper may refer to “entropy” without specifying which entropy, probability distribution, ensemble, coarse-graining, units, and estimator are intended.

# 7. Definitions

## 7.1 Information

**Information** is a family of formally defined quantities describing distinctions, probabilities, correlations, compressibility, causal influence, or encoded content.

IF Theory does not define information as:

- knowledge in general;
- goodness;
- intelligence;
- consciousness;
- energy;
- complexity;
- order;
- meaning;
- or divine intention.

Each use must specify the relevant mathematical definition.

---

## 7.2 Thermodynamic entropy

Thermodynamic entropy is a physical state quantity defined within an applicable thermodynamic or statistical-mechanical framework.

It is not synonymous with:

- visual messiness;
- randomness in ordinary speech;
- algorithmic incompressibility;
- moral decline;
- ignorance;
- or information loss in every context.

---

## 7.3 Informational battery

An **informational battery** is provisionally defined as:

> A physically embodied system’s accessible capacity to produce future work or preserve viable organization because it is away from equilibrium and contains exploitable correlations.

It is represented as a structured object:

\[
\mathcal B
=
\left(
B_{\mathrm{physical}},
I_{\mathrm{accessible}},
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{conversion}}
\right),
\]

where:

- \(B_{\mathrm{physical}}\) is available nonequilibrium physical capacity;
- \(I_{\mathrm{accessible}}\) represents correlations accessible to the system’s mechanisms;
- \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{conversion}}\) specifies the physical mechanism by which capacity can be used.

In restricted isothermal systems, one possible measure of nonequilibrium capacity is:

\[
B_{\mathrm{physical}}
=
F[p]-F[p_{\mathrm{eq}}],
\]

which may be proportional under specified conditions to:

\[
k_{\mathrm B}T
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\left(
p\parallel p_{\mathrm{eq}}
\right).
\]

This equation is not assumed to apply unchanged to every IF model.

---

## 7.4 Battery discharge

**Discharge** is the conversion of accessible nonequilibrium capacity into:

- mechanical work;
- chemical transformation;
- computation;
- transport;
- structural maintenance;
- reproduction;
- environmental modification;
- or dissipated heat.

Discharge does not necessarily mean simple decay. It may create new persistent organization while reducing another accessible gradient.

---

## 7.5 Battery recharge

**Recharge** is the restoration of accessible nonequilibrium capacity through an accounted physical process.

Recharge may occur through:

- external energy input;
- resource concentration;
- gradient creation;
- improved access to an existing resource;
- error correction;
- repair;
- reorganization;
- or coupling to another reservoir.

Intelligence may improve the efficiency with which a system locates or uses capacity. It may not create energy without a corresponding physical source.

---

## 7.6 Structure

A subset \(A_t\subseteq\mathcal U_t\) qualifies as a persistent **structure** when it demonstrates, over a specified interval:

1. temporal persistence;
2. an identifiable boundary or causal partition;
3. stronger or more functionally significant internal interactions than arbitrary comparison partitions;
4. reproducible responses to perturbation;
5. persistence under component turnover or movement, where applicable.

Visual recognizability alone is insufficient.

---

## 7.7 Life

A structure qualifies as minimally **living** within an IF experiment when it:

1. maintains a boundary or operational identity;
2. uses external gradients or resources;
3. maintains a restricted viability distribution;
4. repairs at least some disturbances;
5. preserves organization despite component turnover;
6. propagates or reproduces some portion of its organization.

This is an operational artificial-life definition, not a claim to have solved the philosophical definition of life.

---

## 7.8 Viability

Let \(Y_t\) denote variables essential to a structure’s continued organization.

A viability region is:

\[
\mathcal V
=
\{Y: Y\text{ remains compatible with continued structure}\}.
\]

Possible viability measures include:

- probability of remaining in \(\mathcal V\);
- expected survival time;
- expected future accessible work;
- return time after perturbation;
- organizational persistence;
- or a preregistered combination.

The measure must be chosen before comparing models.

---

## 7.9 Memory

**Memory** is a physically instantiated internal state whose present value carries information about prior states.

Memory alone does not establish agency or reflection.

---

## 7.10 Predictive information

**Predictive information** is internal information about future environmental or system states:

\[
I(M_t;E_{t+\tau}\mid E_t),
\]

or another explicitly defined predictive measure.

Predictive correlation alone does not prove causal use.

---

## 7.11 Causally useful information

Information is **causally useful** when an intervention that selectively removes or scrambles the information reduces a preregistered outcome while preserving relevant alternative properties.

Required comparison conditions may include:

1. intact model;
2. erased model;
3. scrambled model;
4. temporally displaced model;
5. equal-capacity irrelevant model;
6. false predictive model.

---

## 7.12 Causal-work value

The preliminary IF causal-work quantity is:

\[
\mathcal W_C(\tau)
=
W_{\mathrm{intact}}(\tau)
-
W_{\mathrm{scrambled}}(\tau)
-
C_{\mathrm{model}}(\tau),
\]

where:

- \(W_{\mathrm{intact}}\) is work obtained with the predictive model intact;
- \(W_{\mathrm{scrambled}}\) is work obtained after destroying the relevant predictive relationship;
- \(C_{\mathrm{model}}\) is the complete physical cost of acquiring, storing, updating, protecting, and using the model.

A viability version may be defined separately:

\[
\mathcal V_C(\tau)
=
V_{\mathrm{intact}}(\tau)
-
V_{\mathrm{scrambled}}(\tau)
-
C_V(\tau).
\]

Work and viability may not be combined into one scalar unless dimensional and normative justification is supplied.

---

## 7.13 Agency

A bounded structure displays **agency** when internally maintained information causally changes its action selection in a way that improves a preregistered future outcome across more than one environmental condition.

Agency is graded.

### Agency ladder

| Level | Operational capability |
|---|---|
| A0 | Persistence without adaptive feedback |
| A1 | Reactive feedback |
| A2 | Memory-dependent action |
| A3 | Prediction of future environmental states |
| A4 | Counterfactual evaluation of multiple actions |
| A5 | Self-state modeling |
| A6 | Policy revision after model failure |
| A7 | Modeling other agents |
| A8 | Building persistent cooperative institutions |

A system must pass an intervention test before receiving a level.

---

## 7.14 Reflection

**Reflection** is the capacity of an agent to:

1. represent aspects of its own state or policy;
2. represent at least two possible future courses;
3. select among them using expected consequences;
4. revise the policy when predictions fail.

Reflection is not a primitive numerical variable.

---

## 7.15 Functional consciousness

Within IF Theory, **functional consciousness** is a provisional label for an integrated, temporally extended, counterfactual control architecture that:

- represents self and environment;
- coordinates information across competing policies;
- evaluates alternative futures;
- makes selected information broadly available to relevant subsystems;
- revises behavior following prediction error.

This definition concerns measurable function.

---

## 7.16 Phenomenal consciousness

**Phenomenal consciousness** refers to subjective experience: that there is something it is like to be the system.

IF Theory presently has no validated derivation of phenomenal consciousness.

Functional performance may be necessary, sufficient, correlated, or unrelated to subjective experience. That question remains open.

---

## 7.17 Emergent space

Space is **emergent** only when effective distance, locality, dimension, and propagation structure can be recovered from underlying causal relations rather than assumed as an unexplained fixed lattice.

Possible operational distance measures include:

- minimum propagation time;
- graph geodesic cost;
- diffusion distance;
- correlation decay;
- causal-cone overlap.

Multiple independent measures should converge before the model is interpreted geometrically.

An IF universe exhibits **effective expansion** when distances between persistent comoving structures increase according to a defined emergent metric:

a_{\mathrm{IF}}(t)
=
\frac{
\langle d_{ij}(t)\rangle
}{
\langle d_{ij}(t_0)\rangle

Appending empty cells around an array does not by itself constitute an explanation of cosmic expansion.

## 7.19 MaxLove

**MaxLove** is a normative and evolutionary policy hypothesis:

> Prefer policies that preserve or increase the durable, freely exercisable agency of self and others while minimizing unnecessary coercion, destruction, and irreversible loss of future possibility.

MaxLove is not introduced as:

- a thermodynamic law;
- a synonym for fitness;

Scientific IF experiments may test when MaxLove-like strategies outperform exploitation. The moral value assigned to those outcomes requires an additional philosophical premise.

# 9. Core Hypotheses

## H1 — Accessible Battery Hypothesis

Two systems with similar gross energy and ordinary information measures may possess different physically usable capacity because their correlations differ in accessibility to actual mechanisms.

### Falsifier

After controlling for known thermodynamic variables and mechanisms, the proposed accessibility measure adds no predictive value for obtainable work or viability.

---

## H2 — IF Causal-Work Principle

Predictive information becomes constitutive of agency when its interventionally measured benefit exceeds its full maintenance and use cost:

\[
\mathcal W_C>0.
\]

### Strong form

A common dimensionless threshold predicts agency onset across multiple rule families and physical substrates.

### Falsifier

No robust threshold exists, or each substrate requires unrelated definitions and freely selected parameters.

---

## H3 — Emergent-Structure Hypothesis

Resource-conserving local IF rules can produce persistent, bounded, perturbation-resistant structures without explicitly encoding the target structures.

### Falsifier

Persistent structures occur only when their form, fitness, or repair behavior is explicitly embedded in the rule or training objective.

---

## H4 — Expansion–Complexity Window

Persistent complexity is maximized at an intermediate rate of costly causal-space growth:

\[
g_{\min}<g^*<g_{\max}.
\]

### Falsifier

The effect is absent, strictly monotonic, measure-dependent, or disappears under alternative rule families and complexity measures.

---

## H5 — Agency-Threshold Hypothesis

The transition from reactive feedback to predictive agency exhibits measurable critical behavior as predictive benefit exceeds model cost.

Possible signatures include:

- abrupt onset;
- scaling;
- hysteresis;
- critical slowing;
- increased intervention susceptibility.

### Falsifier

No shared transition behavior is found, or the apparent threshold is a direct artifact of the chosen reward function.

---

## H6 — Unified-Geometry Hypothesis

Dark-matter-like gravitational attraction and dark-energy-like expansion may be two regimes of one nonequilibrium informational geometry.

### Required consequence

The model may not independently tune galactic attraction and cosmological expansion.

### Falsifier

Separate functions, couplings, or histories are required to fit galaxies, lensing, the CMB, cosmic growth, and expansion.

---

## H7 — Functional-Consciousness Hypothesis

Integrated counterfactual self-and-environment modeling becomes causally necessary when local reactive controllers face unresolved competition involving delayed or hidden consequences.

### Falsifier

Purely local reactive architectures achieve equivalent performance under the specified conditions, or the global model can be ablated without selective loss of the predicted functions.

---

## H8 — MaxLove Stability Hypothesis

Agency-preserving cooperation becomes evolutionarily stable within a calculable region governed by repeated interaction, repair benefits, reputation, partner choice, innovation, and the cost of exploitation.

### Falsifier

The strategy succeeds only when agency preservation is directly embedded in the fitness reward, or no transferable stability condition exists.

# 10. Global Failure Conditions

The overall IF research program is severely weakened if any of the following persist after reasonable revision:

1. **Metaphor dependence:** central variables cannot be operationally measured.
2. **Free recharge:** information or reflection reduces entropy without accounted physical cost.
3. **Designer leakage:** desired macroscopic outcomes are encoded in primitive rules or reward functions.
4. **Coarse-graining arbitrariness:** emergence appears only under a chosen representation and disappears under reasonable alternatives.
5. **Parameter proliferation:** each phenomenon requires new independent parameters.
6. **No cross-scale continuity:** measures useful in toy universes have no relation to physical, biological, or observational systems.
7. **No unique predictions:** IF fits observations only after flexible retrospective adjustment.
8. **Baseline failure:** simpler established models predict the data equally well or better.
9. **No standard limit:** proposed fundamental dynamics cannot recover successful established physics.
10. **Irreproducibility:** results depend on undocumented code, unstable numerical choices, or selective reporting.
11. **Interpretive inflation:** simulation results are repeatedly presented as evidence for consciousness, morality, or theology without the required bridge.
12. **Immunity to rejection:** every failed prediction is redefined as support for another version of IF Theory.

A research program that cannot die cannot become scientifically credible.

# 2. The Problem with the Original Battery Metaphor

The intuitive battery metaphor contains several different claims that must be separated.

\[
\text{accessible to whom, using what mechanism, over what time?}

- selecting the correct gate;
- predicting the location of a future resource;
- distinguishing fuel from poison;
- coordinating with another agent;
- identifying the efficient sequence of operations;

Information may increase obtainable work without increasing the gross energy physically stored in the environment.

- memory;
- communication;
- computation;

This provides a possible method for identifying agents or organisms without assuming that the programmer’s preferred partition is necessarily the correct causal scale. citeturn775554search3turn775554search8

Recent work has moved even closer to the present proposal. A 2025–2026 physical-intelligence literature includes measures based on goal-directed work per unit of irreversibly processed information, information or empowerment per joule, rare-valid future amplification, and interaction-level predictive structure. citeturn625612academia33turn625612academia34turn625612academia35turn625612academia36

Consequently, IF Theory cannot claim novelty for any of the following statements:

- intelligent systems are physical;
- information processing has thermodynamic costs;
- predictive information is more useful than indiscriminate memory;
- information may enable work extraction;
- agency requires interaction between action and outcome;
- viable systems can contain information meaningful to their persistence;
- intelligence can be normalized by energy or information-processing cost.

The narrower unresolved question is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does a transferable intervention-based physical threshold separate}
\atop
\text{passive correlation, feedback control, and predictive agency?}
}
\]

This paper defines the threshold as a research target. It does not assume that nature contains one universal boundary.

# 2. Scope

This paper addresses **functional physical agency**.

It does not claim to explain:

- phenomenal consciousness;
- free will in the metaphysical sense;
- moral responsibility;
- subjective experience;
- human-level intelligence;
- cosmic purpose;
- divine action.

The initial domain consists of:

- finite-state stochastic systems;
- deterministic artificial environments;
- resource-constrained controllers;
- artificial-life simulations;
- adaptive agents;
- evolutionary populations.

The proposed framework may later be tested in:

- chemical reaction networks;
- active matter;
- microbial behavior;
- cellular regulatory systems;
- robots;
- artificial intelligence systems.

No cross-substrate universality is assumed before empirical demonstration.

# 3. Prior Art and the Novelty Boundary

## 3.1 Thermodynamics of prediction

Still, Sivak, Bell, and Crooks studied physical systems driven by changing environments and separated stored information about past environmental states from information predictive of future states. Their framework relates thermodynamic inefficiency to nonpredictive information retained by a system. citeturn775554search1turn775554search14

IF Theory therefore cannot claim that predictive information is generally more thermodynamically valuable than irrelevant historical memory as an original insight.

The IF extension is to evaluate the information through controlled ablations and quantify the resulting change in net physical work and autonomous persistence.

---

## 3.2 Semantic information and viability

Kolchinsky and Wolpert proposed that information becomes semantic for a physical system when interventions that remove the information reduce a specified viability measure. Their framework explicitly addresses system–environment decomposition, intervention, timescale, and the possibility of identifying agency through semantic information. citeturn775554search0turn775554search6

IF Theory therefore cannot claim novelty for defining meaningful information through intervention.

Its proposed distinction is to keep two effects separate:

\[
\text{causal-work value}
\]

and:

\[
\text{causal-viability value}.
\]

A system might obtain more work while reducing its long-term persistence. Conversely, information might improve survival without increasing exported work. The two outcomes should be reported independently rather than collapsed into a single favorable score.

---

## 3.3 Causal emergence

Hoel, Albantakis, and Tononi showed that appropriately chosen macroscopic causal models can exhibit greater effective information than corresponding microscopic models when degeneracy and noise are reduced at the macrolevel. citeturn775554search3turn775554search8

IF Theory may use causal emergence to identify candidate agent boundaries or macrostates, but it cannot equate higher effective information with agency automatically.

A macrodescription may be causally informative without:

- self-maintenance;
- prediction;
- endogenous action;
- resource acquisition;
- policy adaptation.

---

## 3.4 Empowerment and control information

Empowerment measures the channel capacity between an agent’s actions and future sensory states. Recent physical-intelligence work has explicitly proposed empowerment per unit energetic cost as one axis of physical intelligence. citeturn625612academia33

IF Theory therefore cannot claim novelty for measuring action influence or control information per joule.

The IF intervention asks a different question:

> How much net useful physical output or persistence disappears when a particular internal predictive representation is selectively destroyed?

Empowerment measures potential influence. Causal-work ablation measures the realized physical contribution of a selected internal model.

---

## 3.5 Goal-directed work per information cost

A recent physical theory of intelligence defines intelligence using goal-directed work produced per unit of irreversibly processed information and develops a framework connecting conservation, encoding, computation, and work extraction. citeturn625612academia35

A separate 2026 proposal defines thermodynamic intelligence as the lawful amplification of rare but valid futures and argues that recursive self-simulation is necessary, under stated assumptions, for high performance on that measure. citeturn625612academia36

These approaches strongly overlap with IF Theory’s general motivation. The IF program must therefore demonstrate a specific advantage rather than presenting another renamed work-efficiency ratio.

The prospective distinction is:

1. a matched causal intervention on an internal model;
2. explicit separation of gross battery capacity from model-enabled accessibility;
3. comparison of intact, erased, scrambled, displaced, and false internal models;
4. a search for a transferable threshold across independently designed substrates;
5. automatic detection of candidate agents rather than assuming the agent boundary.

This distinction remains provisional and must survive a deeper formal literature review.

---

# 4. Conceptual Requirements for Physical Agency

A useful definition should not classify every causal system as an agent.

A falling stone causally changes its environment.

A heat engine extracts work.

A thermostat uses information.

A bacterium navigates.

A planning organism evaluates counterfactual futures.

IF Theory therefore treats agency as graded and requires multiple conditions.

## 4.1 Bounded organization

There must be a candidate system \(A\) distinguishable from an environment \(E\) over a stated time interval.

The boundary may be:

- spatial;
- causal;
- thermodynamic;
- informational;
- functional.

It must be selected through a documented rule rather than chosen because it makes the result favorable.

---

## 4.2 Persistence

The candidate system must maintain some organizational identity across time or component turnover.

Let:

\[
V_t
\]

be a set of variables defining its viable organization.

The system must remain within or repeatedly return to a viability region:

\[
\mathcal V.
\]

---

## 4.3 Action

The system must possess internal transitions that alter its coupling to the environment.

Let:

\[
A_t
\]

denote action variables.

An intervention on \(A_t\) must change the distribution of future environmental or system states:

\[
P(E_{t+\tau},V_{t+\tau}\mid do(A_t=a))
\neq
P(E_{t+\tau},V_{t+\tau}\mid do(A_t=a')).
\]

---

## 4.4 Endogenous information

The relevant internal state must be:

- acquired;
- updated;
- preserved;
- or selected

through processes inside the declared system boundary.

A lookup table externally updated with the correct answer at every step does not establish autonomous prediction by the local system.

---

## 4.5 Predictive content

Let \(M_t\) denote an internal model or memory.

It must contain information about a future-relevant variable beyond the immediately available state:

\[
I(M_t;E_{t+\tau}\mid E_t)>0.
\]

Predictive information alone remains insufficient.

---

## 4.6 Causal use

Changing \(M_t\) while controlling relevant alternatives must change actions and outcomes.

The causal path must include:

\[
M_t
\rightarrow
A_t
\rightarrow
E_{t+\tau}\text{ or }V_{t+\tau}.
\]

A representation correlated with behavior but ignored by the policy does not contribute agency.

---

## 4.7 Net physical benefit

The benefit produced by the model must exceed the physical cost of maintaining and using it under at least some environments.

This is the central IF condition.

---

## 4.8 Generalization or adaptation

A fixed reflex may be useful in one environment. Predictive agency requires performance across a defined environment family or adaptation when environmental statistics change.

The system must not receive a newly hand-coded response for every tested condition.

---

# 5. Formal Setup

Let the total system be:

\[
\Omega
=
(A,E,R),
\]

where:

- \(A\) is the candidate agent;
- \(E\) is the environment;
- \(R\) denotes physical reservoirs, including fuel, heat, matter, or work stores.

The candidate agent contains:

\[
A_t
=
(X_t,M_t,U_t),
\]

where:

- \(X_t\) represents internal physical state;
- \(M_t\) is a selected memory or predictive-model state;
- \(U_t\) is the action or control state.

Let:

\[
Y_t
\]

denote future-relevant environmental variables.

Let:

\[
W_{\mathrm{out}}[0,\tau]
\]

be useful work transferred to a declared work reservoir during the evaluation interval.

Let:

\[
C_{\mathrm{sense}},
C_{\mathrm{memory}},
C_{\mathrm{compute}},
C_{\mathrm{communicate}},
C_{\mathrm{act}},
C_{\mathrm{repair}},
C_{\mathrm{reset}}
\]

be physical costs within the same boundary.

Define total cost:

\[
C_{\mathrm{total}}
=
C_{\mathrm{sense}}
+
C_{\mathrm{memory}}
+
C_{\mathrm{compute}}
+
C_{\mathrm{communicate}}
+
C_{\mathrm{act}}
+
C_{\mathrm{repair}}
+
C_{\mathrm{reset}}.
\]

Define net physical return:

\[
\boxed{
J_W(\tau)
=
\mathbb E
\left[
W_{\mathrm{out}}[0,\tau]
-
C_{\mathrm{total}}[0,\tau]
\right].
}
\]

The system boundary must include externally prepared low-entropy memory, remote computation, sensor power, and actuator power. Recent work on physical-intelligence metrics emphasizes that information-per-joule comparisons become misleading when boundary closure, reset, horizon, and imported low-entropy resources are omitted. citeturn625612academia33

---

# 6. The Intervention Family

No single ablation is sufficient. Destroying memory can also change energy, dynamics, architecture, and action capacity.

IF Theory therefore requires a family of matched interventions.

## 6.1 Erasure intervention

Replace \(M_t\) with a default state:

\[
M_t\rightarrow m_0.
\]

This tests dependence on stored state but may alter state entropy and physical cost.

---

## 6.2 Permutation scrambling

Apply a bijection:

\[
M_t\rightarrow \sigma(M_t)
\]

that preserves marginal frequencies but destroys the intended correspondence between memory and environment.

This is useful when labels are arbitrary and action mappings can be held fixed.

---

## 6.3 Cross-episode scrambling

Assign an internal model from another independent episode:

\[
M_t^{(i)}
\rightarrow
M_t^{(j)},
\qquad
i\neq j.
\]

This approximately preserves model complexity and physical representation while destroying episode-specific predictive content.

---

## 6.4 Temporal displacement

Replace the current model with a delayed or advanced model:

\[
M_t\rightarrow M_{t-\Delta}
\]

or, in simulation controls:

\[
M_t\rightarrow M_{t+\Delta}
\]

where future leakage is used only as a diagnostic upper bound.

This tests whether temporal alignment matters.

---

## 6.5 Predictive-variable scrambling

Preserve information about past states while destroying information specifically predictive of future states.

The objective is to produce:

\[
I(\widetilde M_t;E_{t-\tau_p})
\approx
I(M_t;E_{t-\tau_p})
\]

while reducing:

\[
I(\widetilde M_t;E_{t+\tau_f}\mid E_t).
\]

---

## 6.6 Equal-capacity irrelevant model

Replace the useful model with an equally large and similarly costly representation of an irrelevant variable.

This distinguishes model size from predictive content.

---

## 6.7 False-model intervention

Provide systematically inaccurate predictions with matched confidence and computational cost.

This determines whether the policy uses model content rather than merely model presence.

---

## 6.8 Policy-disconnection intervention

Preserve \(M_t\), but remove the causal edge:

\[
M_t\rightarrow U_t.
\]

This tests whether the information is used by action selection.

---

# 7. Definition of Causal-Work Value

Let:

\[
\mathcal I_0
\]

denote the intact condition and:

\[
\mathcal I_k
\]

an intervention.

For each condition, calculate:

\[
J_W^{(k)}(\tau)
=
\mathbb E
\left[
W_{\mathrm{out}}^{(k)}
-
C_{\mathrm{total}}^{(k)}
\right].
\]

Define intervention-specific causal-work value:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W_C^{(k)}(M;\tau)
=
J_W^{(0)}(\tau)
-
J_W^{(k)}(\tau).
}
\]

Because no intervention is perfect, report a vector:

\[
\mathbf W_C
=
\left(
\mathcal W_C^{\mathrm{erase}},
\mathcal W_C^{\mathrm{permute}},
\mathcal W_C^{\mathrm{cross}},
\mathcal W_C^{\mathrm{delay}},
\mathcal W_C^{\mathrm{irrelevant}},
\mathcal W_C^{\mathrm{false}},
\mathcal W_C^{\mathrm{disconnect}}
\right).
\]

A robust effect requires positive values across multiple appropriate interventions.

A single large erasure effect does not establish predictive causal work because erasure may damage the controller nonspecifically.

---

# 8. Gross Benefit, Model Cost, and the Agency Ratio

To study whether maintaining a model pays for itself, separate gross enabled work from incremental model cost.

Let:

\[
W_{\mathrm{use}}^{M}
\]

be useful work with the model.

Let:

\[
W_{\mathrm{use}}^{0}
\]

be useful work under a matched controller lacking that model.

Define gross model-enabled work:

\[
\Delta W_{\mathrm{enabled}}
=
W_{\mathrm{use}}^{M}
-
W_{\mathrm{use}}^{0}.
\]

Define incremental model cost:

\[
C_M
=
C_{\mathrm{total}}^{M}
-
C_{\mathrm{total}}^{0},
\]

excluding work differences already counted in \(\Delta W_{\mathrm{enabled}}\).

Define net model value:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W_{\mathrm{net}}
=
\Delta W_{\mathrm{enabled}}
-
C_M.
}
\]

Where:

\[
C_M>0,
\]

define the candidate agency ratio:

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_A
=
\frac{
\Delta W_{\mathrm{enabled}}
}{
C_M
}.
}
\]

Interpretation:

\[
\Pi_A<1
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{the model costs more than the work it enables},
\]

\[
\Pi_A=1
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{physical break-even},
\]

\[
\Pi_A>1
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{the model produces net-positive work value}.
\]

This ratio is not automatically universal. Its meaning depends on the boundary, horizon, model-free comparator, and definition of useful work.

---

# 9. Viability Must Remain Separate

An organism may spend energy to survive without exporting useful mechanical work.

A predator may extract considerable work while increasing its risk of death.

Work and viability therefore require separate measures.

Let:

\[
P_{\mathcal V}(\tau)
=
P(V_t\in\mathcal V\text{ for }0\leq t\leq\tau)
\]

or another preregistered viability function.

Define causal viability:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal V_C^{(k)}
=
V^{(0)}(\tau)
-
V^{(k)}(\tau).
}
\]

The complete IF result is a vector:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf A_{\mathrm{IF}}
=
\left[
\mathbf W_C,
\mathbf V_C,
I_{\mathrm{pred}},
C_M,
\Pi_A,
\text{adaptation score}
\right].
}
\]

Bits, joules, and survival probabilities are not combined through arbitrary addition.

# 10. Definition of Predictive Physical Agency

A candidate system qualifies as a **predictive physical agent relative to environment family \(\mathcal E\), horizon \(\tau\), and system boundary \(\partial A\)** when all of the following hold.

## Criterion A — Persistence

The system maintains an operational identity or viability distribution over the evaluation interval.

## Criterion B — Endogenous action

Its internal state causally affects environmental or resource transitions.

## Criterion C — Endogenous model

The predictive state is acquired, maintained, or updated within the declared physical boundary.

## Criterion D — Predictive content

The internal model predicts future-relevant states beyond current observation.

## Criterion E — Causal use

Matched model interventions change actions and outcomes through the model-to-policy path.

## Criterion F — Positive net contribution

At least one preregistered outcome satisfies:

\[
\mathcal W_{\mathrm{net}}>0
\]

or a positive causal-viability criterion, with all costs reported.

## Criterion G — Environmental breadth

The effect survives across a declared set of environments or after environmental change.

## Criterion H — No external decision oracle

The correct actions are not supplied by an uncounted external system.

This definition is relative, operational, and graded.

It does not assert that all agents satisfy one sharp metaphysical boundary.

# 11. Agency Ladder

## A0 — Passive persistence

The system persists through fixed physical stability.

Examples may include crystals or static attractors.

## A1 — Reactive regulation

Current sensory input changes present action.

A thermostat may qualify.

## A2 — Memory-dependent control

Past internal state changes present action.

## A3 — Predictive control

Internal state contains future-relevant information that causally improves outcome.

## A4 — Counterfactual control

The system evaluates multiple possible action-dependent futures.

## A5 — Self-modeling control

The model represents aspects of the system’s own future state or limitations.

## A6 — Policy revision

The system changes how it selects policies when its model repeatedly fails.

## A7 — Social modeling

The system predicts the states and actions of other agents.

## A8 — Institutional agency

Multiple systems create persistent shared constraints, records, or coordination structures that expand collective control.

Paper 2 addresses primarily the transition from A1–A2 to A3.

Reflection and higher-order agency are deferred to later papers.

# 12. Analytical Toy Model I: Predictive Resource Choice

Consider two locations:

\[
L,\ R.
\]

One contains a resource worth:

\[
W_F.
\]

The system may choose one location.

A random controller succeeds with probability:

\[
\frac12.
\]

A predictive model succeeds with probability:

\[
q>\frac12.
\]

Let model cost be:

\[
C_M.
\]

Let all other action costs be equal.

The additional expected work enabled by the model is:

\[
\Delta W_{\mathrm{enabled}}
=
\left(
q-\frac12
\right)W_F.
\]

The net model value is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W_{\mathrm{net}}
=
\left(
q-\frac12
\right)W_F-C_M.
}
\]

The candidate threshold is:

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_A
=
\frac{
(q-\frac12)W_F
}{
C_M
}.
}
\]

The model is physically profitable when:

\[
\Pi_A>1.
\]

This threshold depends jointly on:

- prediction accuracy;
- resource value;
- model cost.

High prediction accuracy is not sufficient if the environment offers little benefit or the model is too expensive.

---

# 13. Analytical Toy Model II: Environmental Persistence

Let resource location follow a two-state Markov process:

\[
P(Y_{t+1}=Y_t)=r.
\]

When:

\[
r=\frac12,
\]

the previous location offers no predictive value.

When:

\[
r> \frac12,
\]

the environment has persistence.

An agent storing the previous location predicts:

\[
\hat Y_{t+1}=Y_t.
\]

Its prediction accuracy is:

\[
q=r.
\]

The model is net beneficial when:

\[
\boxed{
\left(
r-\frac12
\right)W_F>C_M.
}
\]

The minimum environmental predictability required is:

\[
\boxed{
r_c
=
\frac12+\frac{C_M}{W_F}.
}
\]

This produces an explicit boundary.

It predicts:

- expensive memory requires a more predictable environment;
- valuable resources support predictive systems at lower predictability;
- in a fully unpredictable environment, model maintenance is wasteful;
- increasing memory efficiency lowers the agency threshold.

This is a model-specific break-even boundary, not yet a universal law.

---

# 14. Analytical Toy Model III: Finite Memory Depth

Let the environment contain temporal dependencies up to order \(K\).

Let model depth be \(L\).

Assume predictive benefit rises and saturates:

\[
\Delta W(L)
=
W_{\max}
\left(
1-e^{-L/\ell}
\right).
\]

Let model cost rise approximately linearly:

\[
C_M(L)=cL.
\]

Then:

\[
\mathcal W_{\mathrm{net}}(L)
=
W_{\max}
\left(
1-e^{-L/\ell}
\right)-cL.
\]

The optimum satisfies:

\[
\frac{d\mathcal W_{\mathrm{net}}}{dL}
=
\frac{W_{\max}}{\ell}e^{-L/\ell}-c=0.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
L^*
=
\ell
\ln
\left(
\frac{W_{\max}}{c\ell}
\right)
}
\]

when:

\[
W_{\max}>c\ell.
\]

Otherwise:

\[
L^*=0.
\]

This predicts a discontinuous onset of nonzero model depth in the simplified optimization problem, although finite populations and stochastic learning may smooth the transition.

# 15. The IF Causal-Work Principle

## 15.1 Weak form

> Internal information contributes to physical agency when matched interventions that selectively disrupt its predictive or policy-relevant content reduce the system’s net physical return or viability.

## 15.2 Cost-aware form

> Predictive information becomes physically self-sustaining when the additional work or persistence it enables exceeds the complete incremental cost of acquiring, storing, protecting, updating, and using it.

## 15.3 Strong threshold conjecture

> Across a meaningful class of resource-constrained adaptive systems, the onset of persistent predictive agency is organized by a dimensionless causal-work ratio near physical break-even:

\[
\Pi_A\approx1.
\]

## 15.4 Universality conjecture

> After appropriate nondimensionalization, different substrates exhibit common scaling behavior near the causal-work threshold.

The weak and cost-aware forms are operational definitions.

The strong threshold and universality forms are empirical conjectures.

---

# 16. Why Positive Causal Work Is Not Sufficient by Itself

A thermostat may use one bit of state to save heating energy.

It may therefore have:

\[
\mathcal W_C>0.
\]

Calling the thermostat a minimal agent may be acceptable under a broad graded definition, but it does not make it a predictive or reflective agent.

The stronger classification requires:

- internal predictive state;
- temporal horizon;
- environmental generalization;
- endogenous model maintenance;
- adaptive policy selection.

IF Theory therefore rejects the equation:

\[
\mathcal W_C>0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{full agency}.
\]

Instead:

\[
\mathcal W_C>0
\]

is evidence that selected information has physical causal value.

Agency classification requires the remaining criteria.

# 17. Simulation Architecture

## 17.1 Environment

The reference environment will contain:

- a spatial or graph-based domain;
- localized energy resources;
- resource depletion and regeneration;
- hazards;
- environmental states with tunable predictability;
- explicit action and transport costs.

Environmental predictability will be controlled through parameters such as:

- Markov persistence;
- periodicity;
- spatial correlation length;
- volatility;
- hidden-state switching;
- observation noise.

---

## 17.2 Candidate systems

The initial experiments will compare:

1. passive dissipative structures;
2. fixed reactive controllers;
3. one-step-memory controllers;
4. predictive finite-state controllers;
5. learned predictive controllers;
6. model-based planners.

All systems must obey the same physical resource ledger.

The model-based systems must pay explicit costs that increase with:

- sensor precision;
- memory capacity;
- model complexity;
- communication;
- planning depth.

---

## 17.3 Endogenous persistence

Systems receive no abstract reward points.

Their effective fitness arises through:

- maintaining internal energy;
- avoiding destructive states;
- continuing to act;
- repairing;
- reproducing where enabled.

Evolutionary runs may select systems by persistence and reproduction, but the simulator must not directly reward “intelligence,” “prediction,” or “agency.”

---

## 17.4 Agent-boundary detection

Initially, system boundaries may be declared for controlled tests.

Later experiments should compare declared boundaries with automatically detected candidates using:

- causal partitions;
- integrated information flow;
- transfer entropy;
- persistent connected components;
- resource-flow closure;
- intervention sensitivity;
- causal emergence.

A result that depends entirely on a hand-selected favorable boundary is weak.

# 18. Experimental Program

## Experiment 1 — Binary predictive choice

Validate the analytical threshold:

\[
\left(q-\frac12\right)W_F>C_M.
\]

Vary:

- \(q\);
- \(W_F\);
- \(C_M\);
- action cost;
- sensor noise.

---

## Experiment 2 — Predictive versus historical memory

Construct memories with matched size and past mutual information but different future-predictive information.

Test whether:

\[
\mathcal W_C
\]

tracks predictive information better than total historical storage.

---

## Experiment 3 — Scrambling hierarchy

Compare all intervention types.

A robust predictive model should show:

- large loss under predictive-variable scrambling;
- large loss under policy disconnection;
- smaller loss under irrelevant-memory scrambling;
- graded loss under temporal displacement.

---

## Experiment 4 — Environmental predictability sweep

Vary the persistence or temporal structure of the environment.

Measure:

- evolved model depth;
- causal-work value;
- survival;
- energy efficiency;
- prediction accuracy.

Test whether model-bearing systems disappear below a critical predictability.

---

## Experiment 5 — Model-cost sweep

Increase the physical cost of sensing, memory, and computation.

Test whether:

- model complexity decreases;
- predictive agency disappears;
- reactive control remains;
- a break-even boundary can be recovered.

---

## Experiment 6 — Environmental regime shift

Allow the environment’s transition law to change.

Compare:

- fixed predictor;
- adaptive predictor;
- reactive controller;
- no-memory controller.

Predictive agency should include recovery after model failure, not merely high performance in a stationary environment.

---

## Experiment 7 — Evolution without agency reward

Initialize random resource-processing structures.

Allow mutation and selection only through persistence and reproduction.

Test whether predictive internal states emerge near the calculated causal-work boundary.

---

## Experiment 8 — Cross-rule-family replication

Repeat the experiments using:

- finite-state Markov agents;
- cellular automata;
- reaction networks;
- recurrent neural controllers;
- graph-based organisms.

The strongest claim requires a common nondimensional relationship.

---

# 19. Phase-Transition Tests

The phrase “agency threshold” should not be used casually.

A smooth cost-benefit crossover is not necessarily a statistical-mechanical phase transition.

To support a genuine transition claim, the program will test for:

- order-parameter behavior;
- finite-size scaling;
- divergent or peaked susceptibility;
- critical slowing;
- hysteresis;
- bimodal state distributions;
- scaling collapse;
- robustness across system size.

Candidate order parameters include:

\[
\langle \mathcal W_C\rangle,
\]

\[
P(\Pi_A>1),
\]

\[
I(M_t;E_{t+\tau}\mid E_t),
\]

and the fraction of surviving systems whose model ablation reduces performance.

A null result—only a smooth crossover—remains scientifically informative.

# 20. Core Hypotheses

## CW-H1 — Intervention hypothesis

Scrambling future-relevant internal information while preserving relevant physical and statistical controls reduces net work or viability.

### Falsifier

Matched scrambling produces no selective reduction.

---

## CW-H2 — Predictive-specificity hypothesis

Causal-work value is more strongly associated with predictive information than with total memory size or information about the past.

### Falsifier

Irrelevant or nonpredictive memories provide equal net benefit after costs and architecture are matched.

---

## CW-H3 — Cost threshold hypothesis

Predictive models persist only when:

\[
\Delta W_{\mathrm{enabled}}>C_M.
\]

### Falsifier

Model-bearing systems remain selectively favored even when their complete physical cost persistently exceeds their physical benefit.

---

## CW-H4 — Finite-complexity hypothesis

When model cost rises with complexity and environmental predictability is finite, an optimal noninfinite model complexity exists.

### Falsifier

Additional model complexity is always net beneficial despite nonzero costs.

# 24. Failure Modes

## 24.1 Hidden oracle

An external process supplies correct actions or labels without its physical cost entering the boundary.

## 24.2 Unmatched ablation

Removing memory also reduces architecture, action capacity, or available energy.

## 24.3 Circular utility

The output measure directly rewards possession of the model.

## 24.4 Cost omission

Training, sensing, external computation, or resetting is ignored.

## 24.5 Correlation mistaken for use

The model predicts the future but the policy does not depend on it.

## 24.6 Action without autonomy

An external controller makes the decisions.

## 24.7 Survival-score arbitrariness

The chosen viability function guarantees the desired conclusion.

## 24.8 Boundary manipulation

The system boundary is changed until the energy ratio becomes favorable.

## 24.9 Substrate overfitting

A threshold exists only in the original toy environment.

## 24.10 Terminological inflation

A simple control improvement is presented as consciousness or free will.

# 25. What Would Count as a Major Result?

## Level 1 — Valid computational measure

The interventions reliably distinguish useful predictive information from irrelevant memory.

This would justify the method but not a new law of nature.

## Level 2 — Robust threshold within one model family

A repeatable break-even boundary predicts when predictive controllers persist.

This would be a publishable artificial-life or information-thermodynamics result.

## Level 3 — Cross-model scaling

The same nondimensional relationship predicts agency onset across independently designed simulation classes.

This would be substantially more important.

## Level 4 — Laboratory transfer

The same relationship predicts transitions in chemical, active-matter, microbial, or robotic systems.

This could establish a new physics-of-agency research program.

## Level 5 — Universal bound

A theorem constrains predictive causal work for broad physical systems, and experiments approach or confirm the bound.

This would be potentially field-changing.

# 26. Novelty Assessment

The current novelty score must be lower than initially assumed because several recent papers propose closely neighboring physical measures of intelligence and agency. citeturn625612academia33turn625612academia34turn625612academia35turn625612academia36

The IF proposal is potentially distinctive only if it demonstrates all of the following together:

1. **Matched model intervention:** the selected internal information is experimentally disrupted rather than inferred from correlation alone.
2. **Net physical accounting:** the benefit is measured after complete incremental physical costs.
3. **Accessible-capacity interpretation:** information changes access to an existing nonequilibrium battery rather than being treated as energy.
4. **Agent emergence:** predictive systems arise without direct intelligence rewards.
5. **Cross-substrate scaling:** one relationship transfers across substantially different implementations.
6. **Clear negative cases:** systems with large information stores but no causal work are correctly rejected.
7. **Prospective prediction:** the threshold predicts a result not used to construct it.

Without those results, the IF Causal-Work Principle is best viewed as a synthesis and experimental protocol, not a novel fundamental law.

# 27. Relationship to Consciousness

Positive causal-work value does not imply consciousness.

A predictive controller may qualify as an agent while lacking:

- global access;
- self-modeling;
- counterfactual depth;
- report;
- subjective experience.

Later IF work may ask whether counterfactual self-models introduce another transition, but Paper 2 makes no phenomenal-consciousness claim.

The correct implication is:

\[
\text{predictive causal work}
\Rightarrow
\text{functional agency evidence},
\]

not:

\[
\text{predictive causal work}
\Rightarrow
\text{subjective awareness}.
\]

---

# 28. Relationship to Free Will

The framework evaluates whether internal models causally alter action and future outcomes.

That can establish a functional form of endogenous control.

It does not decide whether:

- determinism is metaphysically compatible with freedom;
- actions could have occurred differently under identical total physical conditions;
- moral responsibility follows from internal causation.

Those remain philosophical questions.

---

# 29. Relationship to IF Cosmology

The causal-work principle is not a dark-matter or dark-energy equation.

Its relevance to cosmology is indirect.

If IF Theory later proposes that cosmic organization alters access to a universe-wide nonequilibrium state, Paper 2 supplies a discipline:

> No informational contribution may be invoked unless a physical intervention, mechanism, cost, and measurable consequence are specified.

Cosmological structure formation cannot be called intelligent or agentic merely because it generates complexity.

---

# 30. Criteria for Rejection or Major Revision

The IF Causal-Work Principle should be rejected or substantially revised if:

1. causal-work effects cannot be separated from ordinary controller architecture;
2. matched information interventions cannot be constructed;
3. work benefits disappear under complete physical accounting;
4. predictive information performs no better than irrelevant stored information;
5. the threshold is entirely determined by an arbitrary reward definition;
6. evolved predictive systems require explicit intelligence rewards;
7. no relationship transfers across rule families;
8. existing semantic-information, empowerment, or physical-intelligence measures explain every result equally well with less machinery;
9. the concept adds terminology without making new predictions;
10. negative findings are repeatedly avoided by changing the system boundary or agency definition.

---

# 31. Conclusion

Information becomes scientifically relevant to agency not because it is mysterious, meaningful to a human observer, or abundant.

It becomes relevant when it is physically embodied, future-directed, used by action, and worth its cost.

The proposed IF criterion is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W_C(M;\tau)
=
J_{\mathrm{intact}}(\tau)
-
J_{\mathrm{ablated}}(\tau).
}
\]

The corresponding break-even ratio is:

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_A
=
\frac{
\Delta W_{\mathrm{enabled}}
}{
C_M
}.
}
\]

The physical interpretation is:

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_A>1
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{the predictive model enables more useful work than it costs.}
}
\]

That inequality does not, by itself, prove consciousness, reflection, autonomy in every sense, or a universal phase transition.

It supplies a testable boundary between information that merely exists and information that physically pays for its own continued use.

The strongest IF research question is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Across what classes of physical systems does net-positive}
\atop
\text{predictive information become a stable, self-maintaining cause}
\atop
\text{of action rather than a passive trace of the environment?}
}
\]

If no transferable relationship exists, the proposed universal principle fails.

If one relationship predicts the emergence of predictive control across artificial, chemical, biological, and engineered systems, IF Theory will have identified a plausible physical law of agency.
